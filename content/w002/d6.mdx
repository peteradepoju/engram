**The experiment.** 60 V1 neurons are recorded while gratings at 12 orientations are shown, 25 times each, in random order. Each presentation lasts 0.5 s. Responses follow the tuning model from Day 3, with each neuron's own preferred orientation, gain and baseline, and spike counts are Poisson. Your job: recover each neuron's tuning from the noisy data, using no loops over neurons or trials.

<PyCell code={String.raw`
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n_neurons, n_ori, n_rep, window = 60, 12, 25, 0.5
oris = np.linspace(0, np.pi, n_ori, endpoint=False)                 # (12,)
true_pref = rng.uniform(0, np.pi, n_neurons)                         # (60,)
gain = rng.uniform(10, 40, n_neurons)
base = rng.uniform(1, 5, n_neurons)
kappa = 2.0

# Trial order: each orientation 25 times, shuffled
stim = rng.permutation(np.repeat(np.arange(n_ori), n_rep))          # (300,) orientation index per trial
rate = base[:, None] + gain[:, None] * np.exp(kappa * (np.cos(2 * (oris[stim][None, :] - true_pref[:, None])) - 1))
counts = rng.poisson(rate * window)                                  # (60, 300)
print("counts:", counts.shape, " stim:", stim.shape)

<Step n={3} title="Derivation set" minutes={15} />

<Prove title="1. Sum of independent Poisson counts" task="Two independent Poisson neurons have means $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$. Show that the total count $N_1 + N_2$ has mean $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2$ and variance $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2$, so pooling Poisson neurons gives a Fano factor of 1 again.">

Means always add: $\mathbb{E}[N_1 + N_2] = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$.

For independent variables variances add: $\operatorname{Var}[N_1 + N_2] = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$.

So the Fano factor is $(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)/(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2) = 1$. (In fact the sum is exactly Poisson, which you can prove with generating functions in Week 15.) If neurons are correlated, the covariance term adds to the variance and pooled Fano factors exceed 1: an early hint of why correlations matter for population codes (Week 119).

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. How many repeats?" task="A neuron fires at 10 Hz to its preferred stimulus. With 0.5 s windows, how many repeats $n$ are needed for the SEM of the estimated rate to be below 1 Hz?">

Each count has variance $\lambda = 10 \times 0.5 = 5$. The rate estimate is mean count / 0.5, so its variance is $\frac{5}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{0.25} = \frac{20}{n}$.

SEM $= \sqrt{20/n} \lt 1$ requires $n \gt 20$. So at least 21 repeats. This kind of calculation (a power analysis) should be done before an experiment, not after.

</Prove>
