<Step n={2} title="Weekly challenge: decode reaching from motor cortex" minutes={60} />

**The experiment.** 80 motor-cortex neurons are recorded while a monkey reaches to 8 targets, 20 times each. Each neuron is cosine tuned with its own baseline, depth of modulation and preferred direction; spike counts are Poisson in a 0.5 s window. Run the cell to build `counts` (shape: 80 neurons by 8 directions by 20 trials).

<PyCell code={String.raw`
  import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
  rng = np.random.default_rng(1986)
  n_neurons, n_dirs, n_trials, T = 80, 8, 20, 0.5
  dirs = np.radians(np.arange(0, 360, 45))                     # (8,)
  true_pd = rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, n_neurons)

  counts = rng.poisson(rate[:, :, None] * T, size=(n_neurons, n_dirs, n_trials))  # (80, 8, 20)
  print(counts.shape)
`} />

Estimating and testing on the same trials would let noise in those trials help the decoder, so the error would look smaller than it really is. Separating them is the simplest form of **cross-validation** (Week 59), and it is non-negotiable in any decoding analysis.

<Step n={3} title="Derivation set" minutes={10} />

<Prove title="1. Distance from correlation" task="For two z-scored vectors $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ of length $n$ (mean 0 and $\sum x_i^2 = \sum y_i^2 = n$), show that $\lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y} \rVert^2 = 2n(1 - r)$, where $r = \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}$ is their correlation. What does this say about the dissimilarity $1 - r$?">

Expand: $\lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y} \rVert^2 = \lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert^2 + \lVert \mathbf{y} \rVert^2 - 2\,\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = n + n - 2nr = 2n(1 - r)$.

So for z-scored patterns, $1 - r$ is just the squared Euclidean distance divided by $2n$. The correlation distance used in RSA is a Euclidean distance after removing each pattern's mean and scale.

</Prove>

<Step n={5} title="Deep read and critique" minutes={25} />

Give Georgopoulos, Schwartz and Kettner (1986) its third pass and write your critique under the five headings. Focus on one question: **the population vector shows that direction can be read from motor cortex; does it show that motor cortex encodes direction?** Later work argued that motor cortex is better described as a dynamical system generating muscle commands, with direction tuning a by-product (Churchland and colleagues, 2012). Think about what evidence would separate the two views.
