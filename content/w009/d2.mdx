<Step n={1} title="The Jacobian" minutes={25} />

For a function $F$ from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^m$, with outputs $F_1, \dots, F_m$, the **Jacobian** at $\mathbf{x}$ is the $m \times n$ matrix of partial derivatives

$$
J_{ij} = \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j}
$$

Row $i$ is the gradient of output $i$; column $j$ says how all the outputs move when input $j$ is nudged. The tangent-plane approximation of Week 8 becomes

$$
F(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{h}) \approx F(\mathbf{x}) + J(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{h}
$$

so near $\mathbf{x}$ the nonlinear map acts like the matrix $J$ (Week 5), shifted. Its determinant is the local factor by which $F$ scales area or volume.

<Step n={2} title="The chain rule as matrix multiplication" minutes={20} />

If $\mathbf{y} = G(\mathbf{x})$ and $\mathbf{z} = F(\mathbf{y})$, then a small change $\mathbf{h}$ in $\mathbf{x}$ becomes $J_G\mathbf{h}$ in $\mathbf{y}$, which becomes $J_F J_G\mathbf{h}$ in $\mathbf{z}$. So

$$
J_{F \circ G}(\mathbf{x}) = J_F(G(\mathbf{x}))\;J_G(\mathbf{x})
$$

Week 8's chain rule, multiplying derivatives, has become multiplying matrices: Week 5's composition of linear maps, applied to the local linear maps. A deep network's input-output Jacobian is the product of every layer's Jacobian, and **backpropagation** multiplies the transposes of these Jacobians from the output backwards, $\bar{\mathbf{x}} = J_G^\top J_F^\top\,\bar{\mathbf{z}}$, which is exactly what your hand-written backward pass did last Friday.

<Step n={3} title="The Jacobian of a network" minutes={30} />

One step of a recurrent network computes $\mathbf{h}' = \tanh(W\mathbf{h} + \mathbf{b})$. With $\mathbf{u} = W\mathbf{h} + \mathbf{b}$, the chain rule gives entry by entry

$$
\frac{\partial h'_i}{\partial h_j} = (1 - \tanh^2 u_i)\,W_{ij}, \qquad J = D\,W, \quad D = \operatorname{diag}(1 - \mathbf{h}'^2)
$$

The Jacobian is the weight matrix with each row scaled by the slope of that neuron's nonlinearity. Saturated neurons (slopes near 0) wipe out their rows; neurons in their linear range pass $W$ through.

The singular values of the product of Jacobians (Week 7) measure how much the network amplifies or forgets a perturbation over many steps. Last week's vanishing gradients were the scalar version of this; in a real recurrent network, the gradient of the loss with respect to the state ten steps back is $J_{\text{total}}^\top$ times the gradient now.

<Step n={4} title="Linearising around a fixed point" minutes={30} />

For continuous-time rate dynamics $\tau\dot{\mathbf{x}} = -\mathbf{x} + \tanh(W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})$, a **fixed point** $\mathbf{x}^*$ is where the right-hand side vanishes. Near it, write $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}^* + \boldsymbol\delta$. The tangent-plane approximation gives

$$
\tau\dot{\boldsymbol\delta} \approx A\,\boldsymbol\delta, \qquad A = -I + D^*W, \quad D^* = \operatorname{diag}(1 - \tanh^2(W\mathbf{x}^* + \mathbf{b}))
$$

a linear system exactly like Week 6's. Its eigenvalues predict whether small perturbations die away and how fast: a mode with eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $A$ decays with time constant $\tau/|\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda|$. This is the method of Sussillo and Barak (Week 6) and of this week's deep read, in which Mante, Sussillo, Shenoy and Newsome linearised a trained network around its fixed points to explain how prefrontal cortex selects which information to integrate.

<Prove title="The Jacobian of a layer with a bias" task="For $F(\mathbf{x}) = \phi(W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})$, with $\phi$ applied entry by entry, show that $J = \operatorname{diag}(\phi'(W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}))\,W$. What is the Jacobian with respect to $\mathbf{b}$, and with respect to the entry $W_{kl}$?">

Output $i$ is $\phi(u_i)$ with $u_i = \sum_j W_{ij}x_j + b_i$. By the chain rule, $\partial F_i/\partial x_j = \phi'(u_i)\,\partial u_i/\partial x_j = \phi'(u_i)W_{ij}$: row $i$ of $W$ scaled by $\phi'(u_i)$, which is $\operatorname{diag}(\phi'(\mathbf{u}))W$.

With respect to the bias, $\partial F_i/\partial b_k = \phi'(u_i)$ if $i = k$ and 0 otherwise, so the Jacobian is $\operatorname{diag}(\phi'(\mathbf{u}))$.

With respect to one weight, $\partial F_i/\partial W_{kl} = \phi'(u_i)x_l$ if $i = k$ and 0 otherwise: only output $k$ changes, in proportion to the input $x_l$ on that synapse. This is why weight gradients are outer products of error signals and inputs, as you will see with matrix calculus on Day 5.

</Prove>
