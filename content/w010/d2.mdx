<Step n={1} title="The membrane equation" minutes={20} />

Model the patch of membrane as a capacitor $C$ (the lipid bilayer stores charge) in parallel with a resistor $R$ (ion channels that are always open let charge leak out). Writing $V$ for the voltage relative to rest and $I(t)$ for the injected current, conservation of charge gives $C\,dV/dt = -V/R + I$. Multiplying by $R$ and writing $\tau = RC$:

$$
\tau\frac{dV}{dt} = -V + RI(t)
$$

The membrane **time constant** $\tau$ is typically 10 to 30 ms in cortical neurons. This is the equation Lapicque used in 1907 (today's paper is Abbott's short history of it), and the first-order linear differential equation of Week 6, now with an input.

<Step n={2} title="Exact and Euler solutions" minutes={30} />

<Derivation title="Solving for a constant input">
  <div>

With $I$ constant, let $V_\infty = RI$, the voltage at which the leak exactly balances the input. Then $\tau\frac{dV}{dt} = -(V - V_\infty)$.

  </div>
  <div>

Write $u = V - V_\infty$. Since $V_\infty$ is constant, $\tau\dot{u} = -u$, whose solution (Week 6) is $u(t) = u(0)e^{-t/\tau}$.

  </div>
  <div>

So the voltage relaxes exponentially from wherever it starts towards $V_\infty$:

$$
V(t) = V_\infty + (V(0) - V_\infty)\,e^{-t/\tau}
$$

After one time constant it has covered 63% of the way; after three, 95%.

  </div>
</Derivation>

Euler's method is first-order accurate: its error is proportional to $\Delta t$, like the left-edge Riemann sum of yesterday (it is one, applied to the rate of change). A time step of a twentieth of $\tau$ is usually good enough for rate models; spiking models need smaller steps near threshold. Week 21 covers better solvers.

<Step n={3} title="The membrane as a filter" minutes={30} />

For an input that varies in time, multiply both sides by $e^{t/\tau}$ (an **integrating factor**): the left side becomes $\tau\frac{d}{dt}(e^{t/\tau}V)$, which can be integrated directly. Starting from rest at $t = -\infty$, the result is

$$
V(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau}e^{-(t - s)/\tau}\,RI(s)\,ds
$$

The voltage now is a weighted sum of all past input, each moment $s$ weighted by $\frac{1}{\tau}e^{-(t - s)/\tau}$: recent input counts most and input from many time constants ago is forgotten. This is a **convolution** of the input with an exponential kernel, tomorrow's topic. The membrane is a **low-pass filter**: it smooths fast fluctuations and passes slow ones.

<Step n={4} title="The integrate-and-fire neuron" minutes={25} />

Lapicque's model adds one rule: when $V$ reaches a threshold $\theta$, the neuron fires a spike and $V$ is reset to 0, then held there for a short **refractory period** $t_{\text{ref}}$. Between spikes it obeys the membrane equation. For constant input with $RI \gt \theta$, the voltage climbs from 0 towards $RI$ and crosses $\theta$ after a time $T$ that you can find from the exact solution.

<Prove title="The firing rate of the integrate-and-fire neuron" task="Starting from $V = 0$ with constant input $RI \gt \theta$, show that the time to reach threshold is $T = \tau\ln\dfrac{RI}{RI - \theta}$, so the firing rate is $f = 1/(t_{\text{ref}} + T)$. What happens when $RI \leq \theta$?">

From the exact solution, $V(t) = RI(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$. Setting $V(T) = \theta$: $1 - e^{-T/\tau} = \theta/RI$, so $e^{-T/\tau} = (RI - \theta)/RI$.

Taking logarithms, $T = -\tau\ln\frac{RI - \theta}{RI} = \tau\ln\frac{RI}{RI - \theta}$. Each cycle lasts $t_{\text{ref}} + T$, so $f = 1/(t_{\text{ref}} + T)$.

If $RI \leq \theta$, the voltage approaches $RI$ without ever reaching threshold: the neuron is silent. The input current at which firing begins, $I = \theta/R$, is the **rheobase**. Just above it $T$ is very long, so the rate rises steeply from zero; for large inputs $T \approx \tau\theta/RI$ and the rate is limited by the refractory period.

</Prove>

That answers the prediction (closest to every 14 ms). The curve also shows the integrate-and-fire neuron's characteristic shape: silent below rheobase, a steep rise just above it, and a slow approach to the refractory limit.

<Key title="Before you go">
<ul>
<li>The membrane is a leaky integrator: $\tau\dot{V} = -V + RI$.</li>
<li>For constant input it relaxes exponentially to RI; Euler steps approximate this with error proportional to Δt.</li>
<li>In general the voltage is the input convolved with an exponential: the membrane is a low-pass filter.</li>
<li>Threshold and reset give the integrate-and-fire neuron and its logarithmic f-I curve.</li>
</ul>
