<Step n={2} title="Weekly challenge: characterise a retinal ganglion cell" minutes={60} />

**The experiment.** A retinal ganglion cell views a row of 16 pixels, each flickering independently with Gaussian white noise, updated every 10 ms, for 5 minutes (30,000 frames). Its receptive field, hidden from you, has a centre-surround spatial profile and a biphasic temporal profile, followed by an exponential nonlinearity and Poisson spiking. Afterwards, a new 5 s noise sequence (500 frames) is shown 40 times, to test the model. Run the cell to record the data.

<PyCell code={String.raw`
  rng = np.random.default_rng(2001)
  P, L, T = 16, 15, 30000                       # pixels, lags (150 ms), frames
  x = np.arange(P) - 7.5
  k = np.arange(L)
  _sp = np.exp(-x ** 2 / (2 * 1.5 ** 2)) - 0.5 * np.exp(-x ** 2 / (2 * 4 ** 2)); _sp /= np.linalg.norm(_sp)
  _tm = np.exp(-(k - 3) ** 2 / 3) - 0.6 * np.exp(-(k - 7) ** 2 / 6); _tm /= np.linalg.norm(_tm)
  _RF = np.outer(_sp, _tm)                      # hidden: pixels by lags

  def generator(S, F):
      """Generator signal of a space-time filter F (pixels by lags) applied to stimulus S (frames by pixels)."""
      g = np.zeros(len(S))
      for lag in range(F.shape[1]):
          g[lag:] += S[:len(S) - lag] @ F[:, lag]
      return g

  S = rng.normal(size=(T, P))
  spikes = rng.poisson(0.05 * np.exp(1.5 * generator(S, _RF)))
  S_rep = rng.normal(size=(500, P))
  _rate_rep = 0.05 * np.exp(1.5 * generator(S_rep, _RF))
  repeats = rng.poisson(_rate_rep, size=(40, 500))   # 40 repeats of the test stimulus
  print("training spikes:", spikes.sum(), "  test spikes per repeat:", repeats.sum(axis=1).mean())

The STA shows a positive centre flanked by a weaker negative surround, peaking about 30 ms before each spike and reversing sign further back: an ON-centre cell responding to light that has just increased in its centre. Because the receptive field is separable, the SVD splits it cleanly into a spatial profile (a difference of Gaussians) and a temporal one (biphasic). The separability index falls just short of 1 only because of estimation noise in the 240 numbers of the STA.

**Part C: how good is good?** A correlation of 0.78 sounds decent, but even the true model could not reach 1, because the PSTH from 40 repeats still contains Poisson noise. To know how much of the explainable structure your model captures, estimate the **noise ceiling**: split the repeats into two halves, correlate their PSTHs ($r_{\text{half}}$), and correct for using half the data with the Spearman-Brown formula, $r_{\text{full}} = 2r_{\text{half}}/(1 + r_{\text{half}})$. That is the reliability of the full PSTH; the best possible correlation of any model with it is $\sqrt{r_{\text{full}}}$.

<Step n={3} title="Derivation set" minutes={10} />

<Prove title="1. The membrane's response to a single input pulse" task="A membrane $\tau\dot{V} = -V + RI(t)$ at rest receives a very brief current pulse that delivers a charge Q at t = 0, so that $I(t) = Q\,\delta(t)$, where $\delta$ is a spike of unit area. Using the convolution form of the solution, show that $V(t) = \frac{Q}{C}e^{-t/\tau}$ for $t \gt 0$, and interpret the result.">

The solution is $V(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau}e^{-(t - s)/\tau}RI(s)\,ds$. With $I(s) = Q\,\delta(s)$, the integral picks out the integrand at $s = 0$: $V(t) = \frac{1}{\tau}e^{-t/\tau}RQ$.

Since $\tau = RC$, $\frac{RQ}{\tau} = \frac{RQ}{RC} = \frac{Q}{C}$, so $V(t) = \frac{Q}{C}e^{-t/\tau}$.

The pulse instantly charges the capacitor by $Q/C$, the basic relation between charge and voltage, and the charge then leaks away with time constant $\tau$. This impulse response is exactly the kernel of the convolution: the membrane's response to any input is a sum of such decaying steps, one for each moment of input.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The area of a convolution" task="Show that for integrable functions $\int(f * g)(t)\,dt = \left(\int f\right)\left(\int g\right)$. Use it to show that convolving a rate $r(t)$ with a normalised kernel leaves the expected spike count unchanged.">

$\int(f * g)(t)\,dt = \int\int f(s)g(t - s)\,ds\,dt$. Swap the order of integration and substitute $u = t - s$ in the inner integral: $\int f(s)\left(\int g(u)\,du\right)ds = \left(\int g\right)\left(\int f\right)$.

With $f = r$ and $g$ a kernel of area 1, $\int(r * g) = \int r$: the smoothed rate has the same integral, so the same expected spike count, as the original. Smoothing redistributes spikes in time; it never creates or destroys them (Day 3, in continuous form).

</Prove>
