<Step n={1} title="Conditioning" minutes={25} />

The **conditional probability** of $A$ given $B$ is the fraction of $B$'s probability in which $A$ also happens:

$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \text{ and } B)}{P(B)}, \qquad P(B) > 0.
$$

Conditioning shrinks the sample space to the outcomes where $B$ happened and rescales so that probabilities again add to 1. Rearranged, it gives the **product rule**, $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A \mid B)P(B)$, and applied repeatedly the **chain rule**

$$
P(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n) = P(A_1)\,P(A_2 \mid A_1)\,P(A_3 \mid A_1, A_2)\cdots P(A_n \mid A_1, \dots, A_{n-1}).
$$

The chain rule is exactly how a language model assigns probability to a sentence: the probability of each word given all the words before it, multiplied together.

Conditioning is not symmetric. $P(\text{burst} \mid \text{preferred})$ and $P(\text{preferred} \mid \text{burst})$ answer different questions and can be wildly different, a confusion so common in courtrooms that it has a name, the prosecutor's fallacy.

<Step n={2} title="Conditional distributions" minutes={25} />

For two discrete random variables, conditioning on $X = x$ turns one row (or column) of the joint table into a distribution:

$$
p(y \mid x) = \frac{p(x, y)}{p_X(x)} = \frac{p(x, y)}{\sum_{y'} p(x, y')}.
$$

Its mean, $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x]$, is the **conditional expectation**: the best prediction of $Y$, in the squared-error sense, once you know $x$. As a function of $x$ it is the **regression function**, which linear regression (Week 5) approximates with a straight line.

<Step n={3} title="Totals: probability, expectation, variance" minutes={30} />

If the events $B_1, B_2, \dots$ split the sample space into non-overlapping pieces, then the **law of total probability** says

$$
P(A) = \sum_i P(A \mid B_i)\,P(B_i),
$$

a weighted average of the conditional probabilities. The same averaging gives the **law of total expectation**, $\mathbb{E}[Y] = \mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]\right]$, and the **law of total variance**, promised last week:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(Y) = \mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{Var}(Y \mid X)\right] + \operatorname{Var}\left(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]\right).
$$

The first term is the variability that remains once $X$ is known; the second is the variability explained by $X$. Their split is the basis of the fraction of variance explained, $R^2$.

<Derivation title="The law of total variance">

<div>

Write $m(X) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X]$. Then $Y - \mathbb{E}[Y] = (Y - m(X)) + (m(X) - \mathbb{E}[Y])$, using $\mathbb{E}[m(X)] = \mathbb{E}[Y]$.

</div>

<div>

Square and take expectations. The cross term vanishes: conditional on $X$, the factor $m(X) - \mathbb{E}[Y]$ is fixed and $\mathbb{E}[Y - m(X) \mid X] = 0$.

</div>

<div>

What remains is $\mathbb{E}\left[(Y - m(X))^2\right] + \mathbb{E}\left[(m(X) - \mathbb{E}[Y])^2\right]$. The first is $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y \mid X)]$, and the second is $\operatorname{Var}(m(X))$.

</div>

</Derivation>

<Step n={4} title="Conditional independence" minutes={25} />

$A$ and $B$ are **conditionally independent given** $C$ if $P(A, B \mid C) = P(A \mid C)\,P(B \mid C)$ for every value of $C$. The two neurons here are dependent, but conditionally independent given the gain: once you know $g$, A's count tells you nothing more about B's. Many apparent connections between neurons are of this kind, which is why correlations alone cannot establish who drives whom (Week 18 and, properly, the causal inference weeks of Part 5).

<Prove title="Independence in both directions" task="Show that if $P(A \mid B) = P(A)$ (with $P(A)$ and $P(B)$ both positive), then $P(B \mid A) = P(B)$ and $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)P(B)$.">

From the definition, $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A \mid B)P(B) = P(A)P(B)$.

Then $P(B \mid A) = P(A \text{ and } B)/P(A) = P(A)P(B)/P(A) = P(B)$.

So independence is symmetric: if learning $B$ does not change the probability of $A$, learning $A$ does not change the probability of $B$, and the joint probability factorises.

</Prove>

<ul>
<li>Conditioning restricts to the outcomes where something happened and renormalises.</li>
<li>The product and chain rules build joint probabilities from conditionals; total probability averages conditionals back into a marginal.</li>
<li>The law of total variance splits variability into an explained and an unexplained part.</li>
<li>Variables can be dependent yet conditionally independent given a hidden cause.</li>
