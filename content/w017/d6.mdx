**The data.** A neuron is driven by a white-noise stimulus sampled every 5 ms for 100 s. Its spiking follows a Poisson GLM with a stimulus filter (10 bins), a spike-history filter (6 bins) that makes it refractory and slightly bursty, and a baseline of about 20 Hz. The first 75 s are for fitting, the last 25 s for testing.

<PyCell code={String.raw`

Bits per spike is the standard score for spiking models: how many bits better than a constant rate the model predicts each held-out spike. Adding spike history nearly triples it here, because the stimulus-only model cannot know that a spike just occurred and another is now almost impossible.

<Prove title="1. Fisher information of a Poisson GLM" task="For the Poisson GLM with rates $\lambda_i = \exp(\mathbf{x}_i^\top\mathbf{w})$, show that the Fisher information matrix is $X^\top\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})X$, and explain why it does not depend on the observed counts.">

The log-likelihood's Hessian is $-\sum_i \lambda_i\mathbf{x}_i\mathbf{x}_i^\top$ (Day 2), and the Fisher information is the expected negative Hessian.

The Hessian contains only the rates $\lambda_i$, which depend on $\mathbf{w}$ and the covariates but not on the counts $k_i$. So its expectation is itself: $I(\mathbf{w}) = X^\top\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})X$.

Evaluated at the MLE, its inverse is the approximate covariance used in Part B. Bins with high rates contribute more information, which is why bursts of activity pin down the filters best.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. Bits per spike" task="Show that if a model's predicted rate in every test bin equals the constant baseline rate, its bits per spike is 0, and explain why the measure is divided by the number of spikes rather than the number of bins.">

Bits per spike is $(\ell_{\text{model}} - \ell_{\text{baseline}})/(N_{\text{spikes}}\log 2)$. If the model predicts the baseline rate everywhere, the two log-likelihoods are identical and the difference is 0.

Dividing by the number of spikes, rather than bins, makes the score independent of the bin size and comparable across neurons with different firing rates: it measures how much better each spike is predicted. The $\log 2$ converts natural units (nats) to bits, the language of Week 20.

</Prove>
