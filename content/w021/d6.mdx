<Step n={2} title="Weekly challenge" minutes={60} />

The Hodgkin-Huxley model treats the membrane as a capacitor with three parallel conductances: sodium, potassium and a leak. The sodium conductance is $\bar g_\text{Na}m^3h$ and the potassium conductance $\bar g_\text{K}n^4$, where the gating variables $m$, $h$ and $n$ each relax towards a voltage-dependent target, exactly like Day 1's membrane equation but with rates that change with voltage:

$$
C\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t} = I - \bar g_\text{Na}m^3h(V - E_\text{Na}) - \bar g_\text{K}n^4(V - E_\text{K}) - g_L(V - E_L), \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}t} = \alpha_n(V)(1 - n) - \beta_n(V)n,
$$

and the same for $m$ and $h$. The rate functions below are the modern form, with rest at about −65 mV (Hodgkin and Huxley measured voltage from rest with the opposite sign). Units: mV, ms, μA/cm², mS/cm² and μF/cm².

      an = 0.01 * (V + 55) / (1 - np.exp(-(V + 55) / 10)); bn = 0.125 * np.exp(-(V + 65) / 80)
      am = 0.1 * (V + 40) / (1 - np.exp(-(V + 40) / 10)); bm = 4.0 * np.exp(-(V + 65) / 18)
      ah = 0.07 * np.exp(-(V + 65) / 20); bh = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-(V + 35) / 10))

The neuron fires 4 action potentials in 50 ms, each overshooting to about +40 mV and followed by an undershoot to about −75 mV, below rest, as the slow potassium gates close. Every feature of the waveform comes from the four equations: sodium activation $m$ is fast and drives the upstroke, while the slower inactivation $h$ and potassium activation $n$ end it.

The model is silent up to 6 μA/cm² and then jumps straight to about 55 Hz at 6.5 μA/cm², rising only to 85 Hz at 20. It cannot fire slowly. The integrate-and-fire neuron, by contrast, starts from zero rate, steeply but continuously. Hodgkin called these two behaviours class 2 and class 1 excitability, and in Week 23 you will see that they come from two different ways a resting state can lose its stability. (Between about 6.3 and 9.8 μA/cm² both rest and repetitive firing are stable, and which you see depends on how the current is switched on; an abrupt step, as here, kicks the neuron into firing.)

With these pulses the second one fails for delays up to 10 ms and succeeds from 11 ms. After a spike, sodium channels are inactivated ($h$ is low) and potassium channels are still open ($n$ is high), and both take several milliseconds to recover. A stronger second pulse succeeds sooner, which is the difference between the relative refractory period and the absolute one, when no pulse succeeds at all.

<Prove title="1. The Euler stability limit" task="Show that Euler's method for $\dot V = -V/\tau$ with step $\Delta t$ gives $V_{k+1} = (1 - \Delta t/\tau)V_k$, and deduce that the numerical solution decays only if $\Delta t \lt 2\tau$.">

One Euler step is $V_{k+1} = V_k + \Delta t\,(-V_k/\tau) = (1 - \Delta t/\tau)V_k$, so $V_k = (1 - \Delta t/\tau)^k V_0$.

This decays to zero exactly when $|1 - \Delta t/\tau| \lt 1$, that is $0 \lt \Delta t/\tau \lt 2$. For $\tau \lt \Delta t \lt 2\tau$ the factor is negative and the numerical solution oscillates in sign while decaying; for $\Delta t > 2\tau$ it grows without bound, even though the true solution decays.

In a system of equations the limit is set by the fastest time constant, which is why stiff systems such as Hodgkin-Huxley's need small steps or implicit methods (Day 3).

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. A gating variable at fixed voltage" task="With the voltage clamped, so that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constants, solve $\dot n = \alpha(1 - n) - \beta n$ and show it relaxes exponentially to $n_\infty = \alpha/(\alpha + \beta)$ with time constant $\tau_n = 1/(\alpha + \beta)$.">

Collect terms: $\dot n = \alpha - (\alpha + \beta)n = -(n - n_\infty)/\tau_n$ with $n_\infty = \alpha/(\alpha + \beta)$ and $\tau_n = 1/(\alpha + \beta)$.

This is Day 1's membrane equation for the variable $n - n_\infty$, so $n(t) = n_\infty + (n(0) - n_\infty)e^{-t/\tau_n}$.

This is how Hodgkin and Huxley fitted their model: in a voltage-clamp step, each conductance rises or falls exponentially, and the rate and final value at each voltage give $\alpha(V)$ and $\beta(V)$.

</Prove>

<Step n={5} title="Deep read and critique" minutes={25} />

Give Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) its third pass and write your critique under the five headings. Focus on one question: **how much of the model is mechanism, and how much is curve fitting?** Consider that the gating variables and their exponents (the 3 in $m^3$, the 4 in $n^4$) were chosen to fit the shape of the conductance curves rather than derived from channel structure; that the model predicted the action potential's shape and speed, which it was not fitted to; that it describes one preparation, the squid giant axon, at one temperature; what single-channel recordings later revealed about real sodium channels; and why, seventy years on, the same mathematical form still underlies most detailed neuron models.
