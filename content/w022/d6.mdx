<Step n={2} title="Weekly challenge" minutes={60} />

Two populations each prefer one of two choices. Each receives the same input and inhibits the other:

$$
\tau\dot r_1 = -r_1 + F(I_1 - \beta r_2), \qquad \tau\dot r_2 = -r_2 + F(I_2 - \beta r_1), \qquad F(u) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(u - 0.5)/0.1}},
$$

with $\tau = 10$ ms and $I_1 = I_2 = 1$. Models of this kind, reduced from spiking networks, describe how cortical circuits commit to one of two choices (Wang, 2002).

<PyCell code={String.raw`
  tau = 10.0
  F = lambda u: 1 / (1 + np.exp(-(u - 0.5) / 0.1))
  def dec(r, beta, I1=1.0, I2=1.0):
      return np.array([-r[0] + F(I1 - beta * r[1]), -r[1] + F(I2 - beta * r[0])]) / tau
  def jac2(r, beta, h=1e-7):
      J = np.zeros((2, 2))
      for j in range(2):
          e = np.zeros(2); e[j] = h; J[:, j] = (dec(r + e, beta) - dec(r - e, beta)) / (2 * h)
      return J

      k1 = f(x); k2 = f(x + dt / 2 * k1); k3 = f(x + dt / 2 * k2); k4 = f(x + dt * k3)

With weak inhibition the symmetric state, at $s = 0.907$, is a stable node: both populations stay active and nothing is decided. With $\beta = 1$ the symmetric state, at $s = 0.5$, is a saddle with eigenvalues 0.15 and −0.35 per ms, and two new stable states exist, such as the winner state (0.993, 0.007). The saddle's stable direction, the diagonal $r_1 = r_2$, divides the plane: whichever population starts ahead wins.

<Prove title="1. Escape from a saddle" task="Near a saddle with unstable eigenvalue $\lambda_+ > 0$, the component of the deviation along the unstable eigenvector obeys $\dot y = \lambda_+ y$. Show that the time to grow from $y(0) = \delta$ to a fixed size $Y$ is $\frac{1}{\lambda_+}\ln\frac{Y}{\delta}$.">

The solution is $y(t) = \delta e^{\lambda_+t}$. Set $y(T) = Y$: $e^{\lambda_+T} = Y/\delta$, so $T = \frac{1}{\lambda_+}\ln\frac{Y}{\delta}$.

Halving $\delta$ adds a fixed $\ln 2/\lambda_+$ to the decision time, however small $\delta$ already is: there is no limit to how long a perfectly balanced decision can take. In Part B the linear approximation breaks down before the difference reaches 0.5, which adds a constant to every time but leaves the slope, $1/\lambda_+$, unchanged.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The symmetric state of the decision circuit" task="At the symmetric fixed point $r_1 = r_2 = s$, show that the Jacobian is $\frac{1}{\tau}\begin{pmatrix}-1 & -\beta F' \\ -\beta F' & -1\end{pmatrix}$ with $F' = F'(1 - \beta s)$, that its eigenvalues are $\frac{-1 \pm \beta F'}{\tau}$ with eigenvectors $(1, -1)$ and $(1, 1)$, and that the state becomes a saddle when $\beta F' > 1$.">

Differentiate $\dot r_1 = (-r_1 + F(1 - \beta r_2))/\tau$: $\partial/\partial r_1 = -1/\tau$ and $\partial/\partial r_2 = -\beta F'(1 - \beta r_2)/\tau$. By symmetry the second row is the mirror image, which gives the matrix.

A symmetric matrix of the form $\begin{pmatrix}a & b \\ b & a\end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvectors $(1, 1)$ with eigenvalue $a + b$ and $(1, -1)$ with eigenvalue $a - b$. Here $a = -1/\tau$ and $b = -\beta F'/\tau$, so the eigenvalues are $(-1 - \beta F')/\tau$ along $(1, 1)$, always negative, and $(-1 + \beta F')/\tau$ along $(1, -1)$.

The second is positive when $\beta F' > 1$. Then the symmetric state is a saddle: stable to changes in the total activity, unstable to any difference between the two populations, which is exactly what a decision needs. For $\beta = 1$ and $s = 0.5$, $F' = 1/(4 \times 0.1) = 2.5$, giving eigenvalues $0.15$ and $-0.35$ per ms.

</Prove>
