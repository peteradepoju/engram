<Step n={1} title="Phase" minutes={20} />

A stable limit cycle attracts nearby trajectories, so after a short transient an oscillator is always on (or very near) its cycle, and its state is described by a single number: how far round the cycle it is, its **phase** $\theta$. Uncoupled, the phase advances at a steady rate, $\dot\theta = \omega$. When oscillators are weakly coupled, each pushes the others slightly forwards or backwards along their cycles, by an amount that depends on their relative phases. Averaged over a cycle, the effect is a function of the phase difference, and for many systems its simplest form, a sine, captures the essentials:

$$
\dot\theta_i = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N}\sum_{j = 1}^{N}\sin(\theta_j - \theta_i).
$$

This is the **Kuramoto model** (1975). Each oscillator speeds up if others are ahead of it and slows down if they are behind.

<Step n={2} title="Two oscillators" minutes={30} />

With $N = 2$ the model is $\dot\theta_1 = \omega_1 + \frac{K}{2}\sin(\theta_2 - \theta_1)$ and $\dot\theta_2 = \omega_2 + \frac{K}{2}\sin(\theta_1 - \theta_2)$. Subtract to get one equation for the phase difference $\phi = \theta_2 - \theta_1$:

$$
\dot\phi = \Delta\omega - K\sin\phi, \qquad \Delta\omega = \omega_2 - \omega_1.
$$

This is a flow on a circle, Day 1's subject. If $|\Delta\omega| \le K$ it has fixed points where $\sin\phi = \Delta\omega/K$; the stable one, $\phi^* = \arcsin(\Delta\omega/K)$, is a locked state in which both oscillators run at the same frequency with a constant lag. If $|\Delta\omega| > K$ there are no fixed points, $\phi$ keeps turning and the oscillators drift past each other, with a beat frequency of $\sqrt{\Delta\omega^2 - K^2}$. Locking appears through a saddle-node bifurcation at $K = |\Delta\omega|$, and just above it the beats become slow, the theta neuron's bottleneck again.

<Step n={3} title="The Kuramoto model" minutes={30} />

Kuramoto's insight was to measure the group's synchrony with a single complex number, the **order parameter**,

$$
Re^{i\psi} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_j e^{i\theta_j},
$$

the centre of mass of the oscillators drawn as points on a unit circle. $R$ near 0 means the phases are spread out; $R = 1$ means perfect synchrony; $\psi$ is the mean phase. With it the model becomes $\dot\theta_i = \omega_i + KR\sin(\psi - \theta_i)$: each oscillator is pulled towards the mean phase with strength $KR$. The more synchronised the group, the harder it pulls, a feedback that can switch synchrony on suddenly. Kuramoto showed that for a large population with a symmetric, single-peaked frequency distribution $g(\omega)$, synchrony appears at the critical coupling $K_c = 2/(\pi g(0))$. For the Lorentzian distribution of width $\gamma$, everything is exact: $K_c = 2\gamma$ and, above it, $R = \sqrt{1 - K_c/K}$.

At $K = 0.5$, half the critical coupling, $R$ stays near 0.03, the level of random scatter among 1,000 phases. At $K = 1.5$, 2 and 3 it settles at 0.571, 0.702 and 0.812, within 0.006 of the exact values 0.577, 0.707 and 0.816. That answers the prediction: nothing happens until the coupling reaches $K_c$, and then synchrony rises steeply, as the square root of the distance past threshold, the same law as yesterday's supercritical Hopf.

<Step n={4} title="Who joins the cluster" minutes={25} />

Above $K_c$ the population splits. In the order-parameter form each oscillator faces the two-oscillator problem of Step 2 with $K$ replaced by $KR$: it locks to the mean phase if $|\omega_i| \le KR$, and drifts otherwise. So the synchronised cluster contains exactly the oscillators whose natural frequency lies within $KR$ of the centre, and as $K$ grows, $R$ grows, and the cluster recruits more. This self-consistency, the cluster's size set by $R$ and $R$ set by the cluster, is the heart of Kuramoto's analysis.

<Prove title="The locked state of two oscillators" task="For $\dot\phi = \Delta\omega - K\sin\phi$ with $0 \lt \Delta\omega \lt K$, find both fixed points on the circle and show that $\phi^* = \arcsin(\Delta\omega/K)$ is stable and the other unstable. Show also that when they are locked, both oscillators run at the mean frequency $(\omega_1 + \omega_2)/2$.">

Fixed points satisfy $\sin\phi = \Delta\omega/K$, which has two solutions on the circle: $\phi_1 = \arcsin(\Delta\omega/K)$ in $(0, \pi/2)$ and $\phi_2 = \pi - \phi_1$ in $(\pi/2, \pi)$.

The derivative of the right-hand side is $-K\cos\phi$. At $\phi_1$, $\cos\phi_1 > 0$, so the derivative is negative: stable. At $\phi_2$, $\cos\phi_2 \lt 0$: unstable.

When locked, $\sin(\theta_2 - \theta_1) = \Delta\omega/K$, so $\dot\theta_1 = \omega_1 + \frac{K}{2}\cdot\frac{\Delta\omega}{K} = \omega_1 + \frac{\omega_2 - \omega_1}{2} = \frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2}$, and similarly for $\dot\theta_2$. The faster oscillator leads by $\phi_1$ and is held back; the slower one is pulled forwards.
