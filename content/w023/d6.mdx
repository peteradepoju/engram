At the window's edge a stable and an unstable 3-cycle are born together, out of nothing, as the hump of $f^3$ touches the diagonal at three points at once: a saddle-node bifurcation of the third iterate. The convection states lose stability at $\rho = 24.737$, matching the formula, through a complex pair crossing at $\pm 9.62i$: a Hopf bifurcation. It is subcritical: the unstable cycles shrink onto $C_\pm$ as $\rho$ rises, and beyond 24.74 trajectories have nowhere nearby to go but the strange attractor. In fact the attractor already exists from about $\rho = 24.06$, so between 24.06 and 24.74 steady convection and chaos coexist, the same bistability as the Hodgkin-Huxley neuron's.

<Prove title="1. The Hopf point of the Lorenz system" task="The Jacobian at $C_\pm$ has characteristic polynomial $\lambda^3 + (\sigma + \beta + 1)\lambda^2 + \beta(\sigma + \rho)\lambda + 2\sigma\beta(\rho - 1) = 0$. Show that it has a purely imaginary pair of roots $\lambda = \pm i\omega$ exactly when $\rho = \sigma(\sigma + \beta + 3)/(\sigma - \beta - 1)$.">

Write the polynomial as $\lambda^3 + a\lambda^2 + b\lambda + c$. Substitute $\lambda = i\omega$: $-i\omega^3 - a\omega^2 + ib\omega + c = 0$.

The real part gives $c = a\omega^2$ and the imaginary part gives $\omega^2 = b$. Together, $c = ab$.

So $2\sigma\beta(\rho - 1) = (\sigma + \beta + 1)\beta(\sigma + \rho)$. Divide by $\beta$ and collect the terms in $\rho$: $\rho(2\sigma - \sigma - \beta - 1) = 2\sigma + \sigma(\sigma + \beta + 1)$, so $\rho = \frac{\sigma(\sigma + \beta + 3)}{\sigma - \beta - 1}$, which is 24.74 for the standard values. The crossing frequency is $\omega = \sqrt b = \sqrt{\beta(\sigma + \rho_H)}$, about 9.62.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The 2-cycle is born where the fixed point fails" task="Show that the logistic map's 2-cycle points, the roots of $f(f(x)) = x$ other than the fixed points, are real only for $r > 3$, and that at $r = 3$ they coincide with the fixed point $1 - 1/r$.">

The points of a 2-cycle satisfy $f(p) = q$, $f(q) = p$. Subtracting the equations $q = rp(1 - p)$ and $p = rq(1 - q)$ gives $q - p = r(p - q) - r(p^2 - q^2) = r(p - q)(1 - p - q)$. For $p \ne q$, divide by $p - q$ to get $-1 = r(1 - p - q)$, so $p + q = \frac{r + 1}{r}$.

Adding the equations similarly gives $pq = \frac{r + 1}{r^2}$. So $p$ and $q$ are the roots of $u^2 - \frac{r + 1}{r}u + \frac{r + 1}{r^2} = 0$, with discriminant $\frac{(r + 1)^2}{r^2} - \frac{4(r + 1)}{r^2} = \frac{(r + 1)(r - 3)}{r^2}$, positive only for $r > 3$.

At $r = 3$ the discriminant is zero and $p = q = \frac{r + 1}{2r} = \frac{2}{3} = 1 - \frac{1}{3}$: the 2-cycle emerges from the fixed point exactly as its multiplier reaches −1.

</Prove>
