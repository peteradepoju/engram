**Part C: how long does a memory last?** Week 22's bistable population stored a bit in one of two stable states. With noise, the state will eventually hop over the barrier. <Prove title="1. The OU variance equation" task="For $\mathrm dx = -\frac{x}{\tau}\mathrm dt + \sigma\,\mathrm dW$, use one Euler-Maruyama step, $x_{k + 1} = (1 - \Delta t/\tau)x_k + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,\xi_k$, with $\xi_k$ independent of $x_k$, to show that $v = \mathrm{Var}(x)$ obeys $\dot v = -\frac{2}{\tau}v + \sigma^2$ as $\Delta t \to 0$, and find its stationary value.">

Because $\xi_k$ is independent of $x_k$, variances add: $v_{k + 1} = (1 - \Delta t/\tau)^2v_k + \sigma^2\Delta t$.

Expanding, $(1 - \Delta t/\tau)^2 = 1 - 2\Delta t/\tau + (\Delta t/\tau)^2$, so $\frac{v_{k + 1} - v_k}{\Delta t} = -\frac{2}{\tau}v_k + \sigma^2 + O(\Delta t)$. Letting $\Delta t \to 0$ gives $\dot v = -\frac{2}{\tau}v + \sigma^2$.

At stationarity $\dot v = 0$, so $v = \sigma^2\tau/2$, and from $v(0) = 0$ the solution is $v(t) = \frac{\sigma^2\tau}{2}(1 - e^{-2t/\tau})$. The variance relaxes twice as fast as the mean, because it depends on the square of the deviation.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. Why escape is exponentially slow" task="In Langevin dynamics with noise $D$, the stationary density is $p \propto e^{-U/D}$. Explain, using the ratio of the density at the barrier top to the density at the bottom of a well, why the escape rate should scale as $e^{-\Delta U/D}$.">

The stationary density is $p(x) = Ce^{-U(x)/D}$, so the ratio of the probability density at the top of the barrier to that at the bottom of a well is $e^{-(U_\text{top} - U_\text{min})/D} = e^{-\Delta U/D}$.

To escape, a particle must be at the top, and the rate of escape is proportional to how often particles are found there relative to how many are in the well, multiplied by a rate of crossing that depends on the curvatures of $U$ but not on $D$ to leading order. So the escape rate scales as $e^{-\Delta U/D}$ and the mean escape time as $e^{\Delta U/D}$: Kramers' law. Part C's slope estimates $\Delta U$; the prefactor, about $2\pi/\sqrt{U''_\text{min}|U''_\text{top}|}$, explains why it is slightly less than 2 at these noise levels.

</Prove>
