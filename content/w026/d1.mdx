<Step n={2} title="Recap sheet" minutes={20} />

**Arrays (Weeks 2 and 3).** Think in whole arrays: an operation on an array applies to every element, and broadcasting stretches dimensions of size 1 to match. Shapes are the first thing to check. Reproducible work means scripts that run from the raw data, version control and recorded random seeds.

**Vectors (Week 4).** A population's activity is a vector; a neuron's response to an input $\mathbf x$ through weights $\mathbf w$ is the dot product $\mathbf w \cdot \mathbf x = \lVert\mathbf w\rVert\lVert\mathbf x\rVert\cos\theta$. Cosine tuning is a dot product with a preferred direction; the population vector sums preferred directions weighted by rates. Projection of $\mathbf x$ onto a unit vector $\mathbf u$ is $(\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf x)\mathbf u$.

**Matrices (Week 5).** A matrix is a linear map; its columns are where the basis vectors go; multiplication is composition. $A\mathbf x = \mathbf b$ has a unique solution when $A$ is square with non-zero determinant. Least squares minimises $\lVert A\mathbf w - \mathbf b\rVert^2$, giving $\hat{\mathbf w} = (A^\top A)^{-1}A^\top\mathbf b$; ridge adds $\lambda\lVert\mathbf w\rVert^2$, giving $(A^\top A + \lambda I)^{-1}A^\top\mathbf b$. Judge models on held-out data.

<Step n={4} title="Proof practice" minutes={10} />

<Prove title="The normal equations from geometry" task="Show that $\hat{\mathbf w}$ minimises $\lVert A\mathbf w - \mathbf b\rVert^2$ exactly when the residual $\mathbf b - A\hat{\mathbf w}$ is orthogonal to every column of $A$, and deduce $A^\top A\hat{\mathbf w} = A^\top\mathbf b$.">

For any $\mathbf w$, write $A\mathbf w - \mathbf b = A(\mathbf w - \hat{\mathbf w}) - (\mathbf b - A\hat{\mathbf w})$. If the residual $\mathbf r = \mathbf b - A\hat{\mathbf w}$ is orthogonal to the columns of $A$, it is orthogonal to $A(\mathbf w - \hat{\mathbf w})$, so by Pythagoras $\lVert A\mathbf w - \mathbf b\rVert^2 = \lVert A(\mathbf w - \hat{\mathbf w})\rVert^2 + \lVert\mathbf r\rVert^2 \ge \lVert\mathbf r\rVert^2$: no $\mathbf w$ does better than $\hat{\mathbf w}$.

Conversely, if some column $\mathbf a$ had $\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf r \ne 0$, moving $\hat{\mathbf w}$ a little in that coordinate would reduce the error, so a minimiser must have an orthogonal residual.

Orthogonality to every column is $A^\top\mathbf r = \mathbf 0$, that is $A^\top(\mathbf b - A\hat{\mathbf w}) = \mathbf 0$, or $A^\top A\hat{\mathbf w} = A^\top\mathbf b$: the normal equations. The fitted values $A\hat{\mathbf w}$ are the projection of $\mathbf b$ onto the column space, as in Problem 3.
