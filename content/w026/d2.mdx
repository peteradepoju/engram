<Step n={2} title="Recap sheet" minutes={20} />

**Eigenvalues (Week 6).** $A\mathbf v = \lambda\mathbf v$; the eigenvalues solve $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$. With a full set of eigenvectors, $A = V\Lambda V^{-1}$ and $A^k = V\Lambda^kV^{-1}$. Symmetric matrices have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors. Non-normal matrices can amplify transiently.

**SVD and PCA (Week 7).** Every matrix is $USV^\top$: rotate, stretch along axes by the singular values, rotate. The best rank-$k$ approximation keeps the top $k$ singular values (Eckart and Young). PCA is the SVD of centred data; squared singular values give variances.

**Derivatives (Weeks 8 and 9).** The gradient points uphill; the chain rule multiplies derivatives along a path, which is backpropagation. The Jacobian is the matrix of first derivatives, the Hessian of second derivatives; Taylor's expansion $f(\mathbf x + \boldsymbol\delta) \approx f + \nabla f \cdot \boldsymbol\delta + \tfrac12\boldsymbol\delta^\top H\boldsymbol\delta$ links them. A function is convex where its Hessian is positive semidefinite.

**Integrals (Week 10).** Integration accumulates; convolution with a kernel filters a signal; a leaky integrator's response is the input convolved with an exponential.

<Prove title="The gradient of the squared error" task="Using $\nabla_{\mathbf w}(\mathbf a^\top\mathbf w) = \mathbf a$ and $\nabla_{\mathbf w}(\mathbf w^\top B\mathbf w) = (B + B^\top)\mathbf w$, show that $f(\mathbf w) = \tfrac12\lVert X\mathbf w - \mathbf y\rVert^2$ has gradient $X^\top(X\mathbf w - \mathbf y)$ and Hessian $X^\top X$, and conclude that $f$ is convex.">

Expand: $f = \tfrac12(\mathbf w^\top X^\top X\mathbf w - 2\mathbf y^\top X\mathbf w + \mathbf y^\top\mathbf y)$.

The first term has $B = X^\top X$, which is symmetric, so its gradient is $\tfrac12 \cdot 2X^\top X\mathbf w = X^\top X\mathbf w$. The second is linear with $\mathbf a = X^\top\mathbf y$, contributing $-X^\top\mathbf y$. The last does not depend on $\mathbf w$. So $\nabla f = X^\top X\mathbf w - X^\top\mathbf y = X^\top(X\mathbf w - \mathbf y)$.

Differentiating again gives $H = X^\top X$. For any $\mathbf v$, $\mathbf v^\top X^\top X\mathbf v = \lVert X\mathbf v\rVert^2 \ge 0$, so $H$ is positive semidefinite and $f$ is convex. Setting the gradient to zero gives the normal equations of Day 1.
