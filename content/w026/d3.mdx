<Step n={2} title="Recap sheet" minutes={20} />

**Fourier (Week 11).** Any signal sampled at rate $f_s$ is a sum of sinusoids up to the Nyquist frequency $f_s/2$; faster components alias. The DFT, computed by the FFT, gives amplitude and phase at frequencies $k f_s/N$. Power spectra are noisy, so average over segments (Welch). A filter multiplies the spectrum; convolution in time is multiplication in frequency.

**Gradient descent (Week 12).** $\mathbf w \leftarrow \mathbf w - \eta\nabla L$. On a quadratic with Hessian eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, each direction shrinks by $|1 - \eta\lambda_i|$; stability needs $\eta \lt 2/\lambda_\text{max}$ and speed is limited by the condition number $\kappa = \lambda_\text{max}/\lambda_\text{min}$. Convex problems have no spurious minima; stochastic gradients trade accuracy per step for cheap steps.

**Better optimisers (Week 13).** Momentum accumulates a velocity, damping zigzags in ravines. Adam scales each parameter's step by a running estimate of its gradient's size. Constraints enter through Lagrange multipliers: at a constrained optimum, $\nabla f = \mu\nabla g$. Sparsity comes from L1 penalties.

Gradient descent with the best fixed rate needs 386 steps to bring the loss below $10^{-12}$, matching the proof below (the loss shrinks by the square of the error's factor). Heavy-ball momentum, tuned to the problem, needs 72. Adam needs 212 here: its per-parameter scaling cannot fully undo a quadratic's coupling of step size to curvature, and its strengths lie elsewhere, in noisy gradients and badly scaled parameters. That answers the prediction: about 300 steps (the error, not the loss, shrinks a millionfold in about 345).

The Lagrange condition $C\mathbf w = \mu\mathbf w$ says the optimum is an eigenvector, and the variance there is $\mu$ itself, so the best is the largest eigenvalue: this is PCA as an optimisation problem. Projected gradient ascent is the power method in disguise, and with a Hebbian learning rule and normalisation it is Oja's neuron from Week 9.

<Prove title="When gradient descent converges on a quadratic" task="For $f(\mathbf w) = \tfrac12\mathbf w^\top H\mathbf w$ with $H$ symmetric positive definite, eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, show that gradient descent with step $\eta$ converges from every start if and only if $0 \lt \eta \lt 2/\lambda_\text{max}$, and that $\eta = 2/(\lambda_\text{min} + \lambda_\text{max})$ makes the slowest direction shrink by $(\kappa - 1)/(\kappa + 1)$ per step.">

The update is $\mathbf w \leftarrow (I - \eta H)\mathbf w$. In the eigenbasis of $H$ each coordinate is multiplied by $1 - \eta\lambda_i$ per step, so all coordinates shrink from every start exactly when $|1 - \eta\lambda_i| \lt 1$ for every $i$, that is $0 \lt \eta\lambda_i \lt 2$. The binding constraint is the largest eigenvalue: $\eta \lt 2/\lambda_\text{max}$.

The slowest factor is $\max(|1 - \eta\lambda_\text{min}|, |1 - \eta\lambda_\text{max}|)$. The first falls and the second rises with $\eta$, so the maximum is smallest where they are equal: $1 - \eta\lambda_\text{min} = \eta\lambda_\text{max} - 1$, giving $\eta = 2/(\lambda_\text{min} + \lambda_\text{max})$. The common factor is then $1 - 2\lambda_\text{min}/(\lambda_\text{min} + \lambda_\text{max}) = (\kappa - 1)/(\kappa + 1)$.

For $\kappa = 50$ this is $49/51 \approx 0.961$, so a millionfold reduction takes $\ln 10^6/\ln(51/49) \approx 345$ steps. Momentum improves the factor to about $(\sqrt\kappa - 1)/(\sqrt\kappa + 1)$.

</Prove>
