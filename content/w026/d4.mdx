<Step n={2} title="Recap sheet" minutes={20} />

**Probability (Weeks 15 and 16).** Spike counts are often near Poisson: mean = variance = λ, Fano factor 1. Sums of many independent variables approach a Gaussian. Bayes' rule: $p(s \mid r) \propto p(r \mid s)p(s)$; combining independent Gaussian cues weights each by its reliability $1/\sigma^2$. A multivariate Gaussian is fixed by its mean and covariance, and its conditionals are Gaussian.

**Estimation (Week 17).** The maximum likelihood estimate maximises $\sum_i\log p(x_i \mid \theta)$; its variance is about $1/(nI(\theta))$, where $I$ is the Fisher information, the Cramér-Rao bound. MAP adds a log prior, a regulariser. Error = bias² + variance.

**Testing (Week 18).** A p-value is the probability, under the null, of a result at least as extreme. Permutation tests build the null by shuffling labels; the bootstrap estimates uncertainty by resampling. With many tests, control the false discovery rate (Benjamini-Hochberg). Low power inflates published effects.

**Bayesian inference (Week 19).** Conjugate priors give closed-form posteriors (Beta-Bernoulli, Gamma-Poisson). Otherwise sample: Metropolis accepts a proposal with probability $\min(1, p(\theta')/p(\theta))$. Variational inference fits an approximation by maximising the ELBO.

<Prove title="The Poisson rate attains the Cramér-Rao bound" task="For $n$ independent Poisson counts with rate $\lambda$, show that the maximum likelihood estimate is the sample mean, that the Fisher information per trial is $1/\lambda$, and that the variance of the estimate equals the Cramér-Rao bound $\lambda/n$.">

The log likelihood is $\ell(\lambda) = \sum_i(k_i\log\lambda - \lambda - \log k_i!)$. Setting $\ell'(\lambda) = \sum_ik_i/\lambda - n = 0$ gives $\hat\lambda = \bar k$.

The Fisher information per trial is $I(\lambda) = \mathbb E[-\partial^2_\lambda\log p(k \mid \lambda)] = \mathbb E[k/\lambda^2] = 1/\lambda$, so the bound for $n$ trials is $1/(nI) = \lambda/n$.

The estimate's variance is $\mathrm{Var}(\bar k) = \mathrm{Var}(k)/n = \lambda/n$, since a Poisson variance equals its mean. The bound is attained exactly: no unbiased estimator does better, and the standard error is $\sqrt{\lambda/n}$, the formula used in Problem 1.

<Key title="Before you go">
<ul>
<li>Fisher information and the bootstrap give the same uncertainty for a well-behaved estimate.</li>
<li>Many tests need false discovery control; permutation p-values make no distributional assumptions.</li>
<li>Conjugate posteriors are exact; Metropolis reproduces them using only the unnormalised density.</li>
<li>Check coverage: an interval is only as good as how often it contains the truth.</li>
</ul>
