<Step n={2} title="Recap sheet" minutes={20} />

**Information (Week 20).** Entropy $H = -\sum p\log_2p$ measures uncertainty in bits; mutual information $I(S; R) = H(R) - H(R \mid S)$ measures how much a response tells about a stimulus. Plug-in estimates are biased upwards with limited data; correct by shuffling. KL divergence is non-negative and asymmetric; cross-entropy training minimises it.

**Differential equations (Week 21).** $\tau\dot V = -V + RI$ relaxes exponentially with time constant $\tau$. Linear systems $\dot{\mathbf x} = A\mathbf x$ are solved by $e^{At}$, a sum of eigenmodes. Euler is first order and needs $\Delta t \lt 2\tau$; RK4 is fourth order.

**Dynamical systems (Weeks 22 and 23).** Fixed points are where the flow vanishes; linearise with the Jacobian and classify with its trace and determinant. Saddle-node bifurcations create and destroy fixed points (hysteresis, class 1 firing); Hopf bifurcations create oscillations (class 2). Chaos means a positive Lyapunov exponent.

**Stochastic processes (Week 24).** Random walks spread like $\sqrt t$; first-passage times model spikes and decisions. Markov chains have stationary distributions; dwell times are exponential. Drift diffusion explains speed and accuracy. The OU process $\mathrm dx = -x/\tau\,\mathrm dt + \sigma\,\mathrm dW$ has variance $\sigma^2\tau/2$ and autocorrelation $e^{-|s|/\tau}$.

The regression gives a = 0.98997, so τ = 9.92 ms, within 1% of the truth, and σ = 1.000. That answers the prediction: about 1%. The precision comes from the number of independent time constants in the recording, here 10,000 of them in 100 s; with a 1 s recording it would be about ten times worse. This is how membrane and network time constants are estimated from spontaneous activity.

<Prove title="The OU process sampled is an autoregression" task="Over a step $\Delta t$, the OU solution is $x(t + \Delta t) = e^{-\Delta t/\tau}x(t) + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ Gaussian, independent of $x(t)$. Using the stationary variance $v = \sigma^2\tau/2$, show that $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon) = v(1 - a^2)$ with $a = e^{-\Delta t/\tau}$, and that for small $\Delta t$ this is about $\sigma^2\Delta t$.">

In the stationary state, $x(t + \Delta t)$ and $x(t)$ both have variance $v$. Taking variances of both sides, with $\varepsilon$ independent of $x(t)$: $v = a^2v + \mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon)$, so $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon) = v(1 - a^2) = \frac{\sigma^2\tau}{2}(1 - e^{-2\Delta t/\tau})$.

For small $\Delta t$, $1 - e^{-2\Delta t/\tau} \approx 2\Delta t/\tau$, giving $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon) \approx \sigma^2\Delta t$: the Euler-Maruyama noise of Week 24.

So a sampled OU process is a first-order autoregression, and fitting $a$ by regression recovers the time constant as $\tau = -\Delta t/\ln a$. The same logic fits AR models to any recorded signal.
