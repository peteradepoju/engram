<Prove title="C1. Eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix" task="Let $A$ be symmetric with $A\mathbf u = \lambda\mathbf u$ and $A\mathbf v = \mu\mathbf v$, $\lambda \ne \mu$. Show that $\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = 0$.">

Compute $\mathbf u^\top A\mathbf v$ two ways. Directly, $\mathbf u^\top A\mathbf v = \mu\,\mathbf u^\top\mathbf v$. Using symmetry, $\mathbf u^\top A\mathbf v = (A^\top\mathbf u)^\top\mathbf v = (A\mathbf u)^\top\mathbf v = \lambda\,\mathbf u^\top\mathbf v$.

So $(\lambda - \mu)\,\mathbf u^\top\mathbf v = 0$, and since $\lambda \ne \mu$, $\mathbf u^\top\mathbf v = 0$. This is why covariance matrices have orthogonal principal directions.

</Prove>

<Prove title="C2. Why random walks spread like the square root of time" task="Let $X_n = \sum_{k=1}^n\xi_k$ with the $\xi_k$ independent, mean 0 and variance $s^2$. Show that $\mathrm{Var}(X_n) = ns^2$, starting from $\mathrm{Var}(X + Y) = \mathrm{Var}(X) + \mathrm{Var}(Y) + 2\,\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y)$.">

For independent variables the covariance is zero: $\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y) = \mathbb E[XY] - \mathbb E[X]\mathbb E[Y] = 0$ because $\mathbb E[XY] = \mathbb E[X]\mathbb E[Y]$. So variances of independent variables add.

By induction, $\mathrm{Var}(X_n) = \mathrm{Var}(X_{n-1}) + \mathrm{Var}(\xi_n) = (n - 1)s^2 + s^2 = ns^2$, since $\xi_n$ is independent of $X_{n-1}$, which depends only on earlier steps.

The standard deviation is $s\sqrt n$: the square-root law behind random walks, diffusion, the standard error of a mean and the noise term $\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}$ in every stochastic simulation of Part 1.

</Prove>
