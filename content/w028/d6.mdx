From the same activity, the spike counts identify every trial's stimulus (100%), the noisier calcium-like signal 87%, and the single voxel only 42%, against chance at 25%. The voxel loses most of the information not because it is noisy but because it sums neurons with different preferences, so the stimulus-specific pattern averages away; what is left is the small difference in total activity between stimuli. An absent BOLD effect can therefore hide rich information in the neurons beneath it.

<Prove title="1. Random phases add up to the square root of N" task="Let $\phi_1, \dots, \phi_N$ be independent and uniform on $[0, 2\pi)$. Show that $\mathbb E\left|\sum_k e^{i\phi_k}\right|^2 = N$.">

Expand the squared modulus as a double sum: $\left|\sum_k e^{i\phi_k}\right|^2 = \sum_j\sum_k e^{i(\phi_j - \phi_k)}$.

The $N$ terms with $j = k$ each equal 1. For $j \ne k$, independence gives $\mathbb E[e^{i\phi_j}]\,\mathbb E[e^{-i\phi_k}]$, and $\mathbb E[e^{i\phi}] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i\phi}\,\mathrm d\phi = 0$, so those terms vanish.

Hence the expectation is $N$, and the root-mean-square amplitude is $\sqrt N$, against $N$ for sources in phase: the same square-root law as the random walk, now for phases.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. Deconvolution inverts the calcium filter" task="If $c_t = \gamma c_{t-1} + s_t$ and the fluorescence has no noise, $F_t = c_t$, show that $F_t - \gamma F_{t-1} = s_t$ exactly, and that with noise $\varepsilon_t$ of variance $\sigma^2$ the estimate's error has variance $(1 + \gamma^2)\sigma^2$.">

Substitute: $F_t - \gamma F_{t-1} = c_t - \gamma c_{t-1} = s_t$, by the definition of $c_t$.

With noise, $F_t - \gamma F_{t-1} = s_t + \varepsilon_t - \gamma\varepsilon_{t-1}$. The error $\varepsilon_t - \gamma\varepsilon_{t-1}$ is a sum of independent terms, with variance $\sigma^2 + \gamma^2\sigma^2$.

With $\gamma$ close to 1 (slow indicators, fast imaging), the error variance nearly doubles: deconvolution sharpens the signal in time at the price of amplifying noise, which is why practical methods add priors such as sparsity and non-negativity.

</Prove>
