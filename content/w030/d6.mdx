  r = np.random.default_rng(1952)

  Vs = np.arange(-80, 51, 10.0)

  taus = np.linspace(0.2, 12, 1181)
  ninf_list, tau_list = [], []
  for g in data:
      ni = max(g[-40:].mean(), 1e-6) ** 0.25 / gK ** 0.25

<Prove title="1. Why a power gives a delay" task="Starting from $n(0) = 0$, the gate rises as $n(t) = n_\infty(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$. Show that for $t \ll \tau$ the conductance $\bar g n^p$ grows like $t^p$, so that its first $p - 1$ time derivatives vanish at $t = 0$.">

For small $t$, $1 - e^{-t/\tau} = t/\tau - t^2/(2\tau^2) + \dots \approx t/\tau$ (Week 9's Taylor series).

So $n(t) \approx n_\infty t/\tau$ and $\bar g n^p \approx \bar g (n_\infty/\tau)^p t^p$.

A function that starts like $t^p$ has its first $p - 1$ derivatives equal to zero at the origin: it leaves zero flat, with a delay that is more pronounced the larger $p$ is. With $p = 1$ the conductance would rise at full speed immediately, which the potassium records did not.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. Temperature changes shape, not just speed" task="Multiply every rate in the HH model by $\phi$. Show that the gating equations in time $t$ are identical to the original gating equations in rescaled time $s = \phi t$, but that the membrane equation is not, and explain why warming therefore changes the spike's shape.">

Each gating equation becomes $\frac{dx}{dt} = \phi[\alpha(V)(1 - x) - \beta(V)x]$. With $s = \phi t$, $\frac{dx}{ds} = \frac{1}{\phi}\frac{dx}{dt} = \alpha(1 - x) - \beta x$: the original equation.

The membrane equation $C\frac{dV}{dt} = I - I_{ion}$ becomes $C\phi\frac{dV}{ds} = I - I_{ion}$ in the new time: it is as if the capacitance had been multiplied by $\phi$.

So warming by $\phi$ is equivalent to keeping the gates as they were and making the membrane $\phi$ times slower to charge. The gates now outrun the voltage, which is why the spike shrinks, and above a certain $\phi$ the sodium current inactivates before it can charge the membrane: the heat block of Day 4.

</Prove>
