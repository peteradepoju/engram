The true neuron's Fano factor in active windows is about 0.70, well below the Poisson value of 1. The GLM simulator gives about 0.72; the LNP simulator gives 1.01, Poisson by construction, despite having nearly the same PSTH (correlations with the data of 0.91 and 0.87). The rasters show why: after each spike the data and the GLM are silenced by refractoriness, so the number of spikes in an event varies little from repeat to repeat, while the LNP's spikes ignore one another and their count varies as much as its mean. This is in miniature the result of Pillow and colleagues (2005), whose GLM simulations reproduced both the PSTH and the trial-to-trial precision of primate RGCs, where a Poisson model with the right rate could not.

<Prove title="1. The Poisson GLM log-likelihood is concave" task="With $\lambda_t = \exp(x_t \cdot w)$ and $\mathcal L(w) = \sum_t (y_t\, x_t \cdot w - \lambda_t)$, show that the Hessian is $-X^\top \text{diag}(\lambda) X$ and hence that $\mathcal L$ is concave.">

The gradient is $\nabla \mathcal L = \sum_t (y_t - \lambda_t)\, x_t = X^\top (y - \lambda)$, because $\partial \lambda_t / \partial w = \lambda_t x_t$.

Differentiating again, $\nabla^2 \mathcal L = -\sum_t \lambda_t\, x_t x_t^\top = -X^\top \text{diag}(\lambda) X$.

For any vector $v$, $v^\top \nabla^2 \mathcal L\, v = -\sum_t \lambda_t (x_t \cdot v)^2 \le 0$, since every $\lambda_t$ is positive. The Hessian is negative semi-definite everywhere, so $\mathcal L$ is concave and any local maximum is global. This holds whatever the columns of $X$ contain, stimulus, spike history or other neurons' spikes, because the history enters linearly as data.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The STA recovers the filter for Gaussian white noise" task="Let $s \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$ and let the spike probability be $f(k \cdot s)$ for any non-negative $f$ with finite expectation. Show that the spike-triggered average is proportional to $k$.">

The STA is $\mathbb E[s \mid \text{spike}] = \mathbb E[s\, f(k \cdot s)] / \mathbb E[f(k \cdot s)]$.

Write $s = \frac{k}{|k|^2}(k \cdot s) + s_\perp$, where $s_\perp$ is the part orthogonal to $k$. For a Gaussian with identity covariance, $s_\perp$ is independent of $k \cdot s$ and has mean zero.

Hence $\mathbb E[s_\perp f(k \cdot s)] = \mathbb E[s_\perp]\,\mathbb E[f(k \cdot s)] = 0$, and $\mathbb E[s\, f(k \cdot s)] = \frac{k}{|k|^2}\mathbb E[(k \cdot s) f(k \cdot s)]$, a multiple of $k$. The multiple is zero when $f$ is even, which is why Day 2 needed the covariance. With correlated stimuli, $s_\perp$ is no longer independent, and Day 1's whitening restores the result.

</Prove>
