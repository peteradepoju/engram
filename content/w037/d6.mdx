<Prove title="1. The design effect" task="Neurons $j = 1, \dots, m$ in one animal have values $x_j = a + e_j$, with a shared animal effect $a$ of variance $\sigma_a^2$ and independent $e_j$ of variance $\sigma_e^2$. Show that the variance of their mean is $\frac{\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_e^2}{m}\left(1 + (m - 1)\rho\right)$, with $\rho = \sigma_a^2/(\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_e^2)$.">

The mean is $\bar x = a + \frac{1}{m}\sum_j e_j$, and the two parts are independent, so $\text{Var}(\bar x) = \sigma_a^2 + \sigma_e^2/m$.

Write $\sigma^2 = \sigma_a^2 + \sigma_e^2$, so that $\sigma_a^2 = \rho\sigma^2$ and $\sigma_e^2 = (1 - \rho)\sigma^2$. Then $\text{Var}(\bar x) = \rho\sigma^2 + (1 - \rho)\sigma^2/m = \frac{\sigma^2}{m}\left(m\rho + 1 - \rho\right)$.

That is $\frac{\sigma^2}{m}(1 + (m - 1)\rho)$: the variance of a mean of $m$ independent values times the design effect. As $m$ grows it tends to $\rho\sigma^2$, the animal's own variance, which no number of neurons can average away.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The violation metric" task="A unit's $N$ spikes in time $T$ are a refractory neuron's (fraction $1 - f$) plus unrelated Poisson spikes (fraction $f$). Ignoring edge effects and assuming $t_r$ is short, show that violations within $t_r$ number about $\frac{t_r N^2}{T}(2f - f^2)$, so that $m = \text{violations}/(2 t_r N^2/T) = f - f^2/2$.">

A violation needs two spikes within $t_r$ of each other, and two of the neuron's own spikes never are. For two independent processes with rates $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, the expected number of pairs within $t_r$ is $2 t_r \lambda_1 \lambda_2 T$, and for one Poisson process with itself it is $t_r \lambda^2 T$.

With $\lambda = N/T$, contaminant with neuron gives $2 t_r f(1 - f)\lambda^2 T$, and contaminant with contaminant gives $t_r f^2 \lambda^2 T$. The total is $t_r \lambda^2 T (2f - 2f^2 + f^2) = \frac{t_r N^2}{T}(2f - f^2)$.

Dividing by $2 t_r N^2/T$ gives $m = f - f^2/2$, which is 0.5 when $f = 1$. Solving the quadratic for the root below 1 gives $f = 1 - \sqrt{1 - 2m}$.

</Prove>
