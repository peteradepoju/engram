<Step n={1} title="Two streams" minutes={20} />

Ungerleider and Mishkin (1982) proposed, from lesion studies in monkeys, that visual cortex divides into a **ventral** stream running into the temporal lobe, needed to recognise objects, and a **dorsal** stream running into the parietal lobe, needed to locate them; Goodale and Milner later recast the dorsal stream as vision for action. Along the ventral stream, from V1 through V2 and V4 to inferior temporal cortex (IT), receptive fields grow from about a degree to tens of degrees, response latencies lengthen, and preferred stimuli become more complex: oriented edges in V1, corners and curvature in V2 and V4, object parts and whole objects, including faces, in IT. Yet IT neurons respond to their preferred objects across a range of positions and sizes, the **invariance**, or better tolerance, that recognition needs.

Riesenhuber and Poggio's HMAX model builds this with two kinds of layer. **S** (simple) layers compute selectivity: each unit matches its input against a template, as a V1 simple cell matches the image against a Gabor. **C** (complex) layers compute tolerance: each unit takes the maximum over S units with the same template at nearby positions or scales, as a complex cell pools over phase and position. Stacking S and C layers gives units that are both more selective and more tolerant at each level.

<Step n={4} title="Selectivity from conjunctions" minutes={30} />

A T and an upside-down T contain exactly the same bars; they differ only in how the bars are arranged. Global pooling of orientation throws the arrangement away. HMAX's answer is a second S layer: **S2** units match small patches of the C1 maps, which span several orientations at neighbouring positions, against stored templates. A template taken from the junction of a T responds to a horizontal bar above a vertical one, a conjunction of features. A **C2** layer then takes the maximum of each S2 unit over all positions, so the conjunction is detected wherever it occurs.

Global orientation pooling is at chance for T against upside-down T (45%), which answers the prediction: no, because the two shapes contain the same horizontal and vertical bars. The C2 features, built from twelve templates of C1 patches taken from one centred example of each shape, classify every test image correctly at positions they were never trained on. Selectivity for arrangement came from the templates; tolerance to position came from taking their maximum over the image. Stacking more such stages, with templates for conjunctions of conjunctions, is the route from edges to objects.

<Callout kind="think">

HMAX is a convolutional network with hand-set first-layer filters, max pooling and templates copied from examples. Deep convolutional networks keep the architecture and learn every layer by gradient descent on object recognition, and Week 35 showed that their layers predict V4 and IT responses well. Ask what HMAX still offers: an interpretable account of why the architecture works (alternating selectivity and tolerance), which the trained networks share but do not explain.
