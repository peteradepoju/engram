Each fixed gain is good in one ensemble only: a gain of 1 carries 2.4 bits in low-variance blocks but 1.7 in high-variance ones, where its response saturates, and a gain of 4 does the reverse. The adaptive gain carries about 2.4 bits in both, the best mean. Over the whole recording, though, it carries only 1.8 bits against 2.45 for the compromise gain of 2: by rescaling to each ensemble it makes a small stimulus in the quiet blocks look like a large one in the loud blocks, discarding the absolute scale. This is the trade-off Brenner and colleagues and Fairhall and colleagues explored in the fly.

With the same output power, the unfiltered channel carries about 10 bits, pure whitening about 15, and the Atick and Redlich filter about 22. Without filtering, most of the power goes to the strong low frequencies, which needed little; whitening spreads it evenly, including over high frequencies where the input is mostly noise; the Atick and Redlich filter spends it where signal exceeds noise. The ranking holds for any power budget you try, which is the quantitative content of yesterday's centre-surround prediction.

<Prove title="1. Information of a Gaussian channel" task="A Gaussian signal of variance $\sigma_s^2$ is received with independent Gaussian noise of variance $\sigma_n^2$. Show that the mutual information is $\frac12\log_2(1 + \sigma_s^2/\sigma_n^2)$ bits.">

The differential entropy of a Gaussian of variance $v$ is $\frac12\log_2(2\pi e v)$.

The received value $r = s + n$ has variance $\sigma_s^2 + \sigma_n^2$, and given $s$ its uncertainty is the noise alone. So $I = h(r) - h(r \mid s) = \frac12\log_2(2\pi e(\sigma_s^2 + \sigma_n^2)) - \frac12\log_2(2\pi e\sigma_n^2)$.

The constants cancel, leaving $\frac12\log_2(1 + \sigma_s^2/\sigma_n^2)$. Summing over independent frequency components gives Part B's formula, with signal power $K^2 S$ and noise $K^2 N + M$ at each frequency.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The Atick and Redlich filter's two limits" task="With $K(f) = \frac{S}{S+N}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{S+N}}$, show that $K \approx S^{-1/2}$ when $S \gg N$ and $K \approx S/N^{3/2}$ when $S \ll N$, and say what each means for a $1/f^2$ spectrum.">

When $S \gg N$, $S/(S+N) \approx 1$ and $\sqrt{S+N} \approx \sqrt S$, so $K \approx S^{-1/2}$. For $S \propto 1/f^2$ this is $K \propto f$: gain rising with frequency, which whitens.

When $S \ll N$, $S/(S+N) \approx S/N$ and $\sqrt{S+N} \approx \sqrt N$, so $K \approx S/N^{3/2} \propto 1/f^2$: gain falling with frequency, which smooths.

The filter rises at low frequencies and falls at high ones, a band-pass filter peaking where $S \approx N$; in space, a centre with a surround. As $N$ grows the peak moves to lower frequencies, the centre widens and the surround fades.

</Prove>
