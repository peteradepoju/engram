      alpha, beta = par[0], par[1]; kappa = par[2] if sticky else 0.0
      Q = np.zeros(2); prev = -1; L = 0.0

  g = np.random.default_rng(7)
  xs = np.where(g.random(200000) < 0.3, g.normal(1, 0.3, 200000), g.normal(6, 0.8, 200000))
  taus = np.linspace(0.05, 0.95, 19)

  def expectiles(samples):

      for tau in taus:
          lo, hi = samples.min(), samples.max()
          for _ in range(40):

              lo, hi = (m, hi) if f > 0 else (lo, m)
          out.append(m)
      return np.array(out)

  E = expectiles(xs)                       # the "reversal points" of nineteen neurons

      f = taus * np.mean(np.maximum(d, 0), 1) - (1 - taus) * np.mean(np.maximum(-d, 0), 1)

The optimiser finds 50 samples whose expectiles match the nineteen reversal points almost exactly. They put 30% of their mass below 3.5, as the true distribution does, and their mean is 4.50. The decoded histogram has two groups, but their shapes differ from the truth: nineteen numbers cannot pin down fifty, so many sample sets are equally consistent. Dabney and colleagues faced the same limitation with real neurons and fewer reward sizes, which is why their decoded distributions are best read as capturing coarse features such as bimodality rather than fine shape.

<Step n={3} title="Derivation set" minutes={10} />

<Prove title="1. Rescorla-Wagner after n trials" task="Starting from $V_0 = 0$ with reward 1 on every trial, show that $V_n = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^n$.">

The update is $V_{n} = V_{n-1} + \alpha(1 - V_{n-1})$, so $1 - V_n = (1 - \alpha)(1 - V_{n-1})$.

The distance from 1 shrinks by the factor $1 - \alpha$ each trial, so $1 - V_n = (1 - \alpha)^n(1 - V_0) = (1 - \alpha)^n$.

Hence $V_n = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^n$, the negatively accelerated learning curve. The number of trials to get halfway is $\ln 2/|\ln(1 - \alpha)| \approx 0.69/\alpha$ for small $\alpha$.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The value of a TD cue with probabilistic reward" task="With the complete serial compound, $\gamma = 1$ and reward 1 delivered with probability $p$ at a fixed time after the cue, show that the expected TD update is zero when every post-cue weight equals $p$, and hence that the error at the cue converges to $p$ on average.">

After the cue, consecutive post-cue states have equal values $p$, so for steps before the reward $\delta = 0 + p - p = 0$.

At the step into the reward, $\delta = r - p$, whose expectation is $p \cdot 1 - p = 0$. So the weights $p$ are a fixed point of the expected update.

Before the cue the value is zero, so the transition into the cue gives $\delta = 0 + p - 0 = p$. The cue response therefore converges to $p$, and the response to a delivered reward to $1 - p$, as in Day 2.

</Prove>
