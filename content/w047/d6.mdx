<Prove title="1. The PPC is unbiased" task="For $n$ independent phases drawn uniformly, show that $\mathbb E\left[\left|\sum_k e^{i\phi_k}\right|^2\right] = n$, and hence that the expected PPC is zero.">

$\left|\sum_k e^{i\phi_k}\right|^2 = \sum_j\sum_k e^{i(\phi_j - \phi_k)} = n + \sum_{j \neq k} e^{i(\phi_j - \phi_k)}$, since the $n$ terms with $j = k$ each equal 1.

For $j \neq k$, the phases are independent and uniform, so $\mathbb E[e^{i(\phi_j - \phi_k)}] = \mathbb E[e^{i\phi_j}]\,\mathbb E[e^{-i\phi_k}] = 0 \times 0 = 0$.

Hence the expectation is $n$, and $\mathbb E[\text{PPC}] = (n - n)/(n(n - 1)) = 0$. The PLV squared, $|\sum e^{i\phi}|^2/n^2$, has expectation $1/n$ instead: the self-pairs are the bias.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. Gain of a coherent and an incoherent sender" task="A receiver's excitability is $\frac12(1 + \cos\theta)$. Show that spikes arriving at a uniformly random phase have mean gain $\frac12$, and spikes arriving at phases with a normal spread of standard deviation $s$ around $\theta = 0$ have mean gain $\frac12(1 + e^{-s^2/2})$.">

For uniform $\theta$, $\mathbb E[\cos\theta] = 0$, so the mean gain is $\frac12$.

For $\theta \sim \mathcal N(0, s^2)$, $\mathbb E[\cos\theta] = \operatorname{Re}\,\mathbb E[e^{i\theta}] = e^{-s^2/2}$, the characteristic function of the normal distribution at 1.

So the mean gain is $\frac12(1 + e^{-s^2/2})$, which is 1 when spikes are perfectly timed and falls towards $\frac12$ as jitter grows. With 10 ms of jitter at 40 Hz, $s = 2\pi \times 40 \times 0.01 \approx 2.5$ rad, and the gain is about 0.52, as in Day 3.

</Prove>
