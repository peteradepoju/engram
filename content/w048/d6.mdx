For the observer with a prior, adding the slope parameter improves the fit by a likelihood ratio of about 41 (p around $10^{-10}$), and the fitted slope, $-1.4$, is close to the true $-1.2$. For the observer without one, the ratio is essentially zero and the fitted slope is near 0. Because the models are nested, this test is cleaner than comparing AIC or BIC: under the null hypothesis the statistic has a known distribution. Its weakness is that it only asks whether the prior improves this particular model; if the model is wrong in another way, the extra parameter may absorb the misfit.

<Prove title="1. The posterior mean with Gaussian prior and likelihood" task="With likelihood $\mathcal N(m, \sigma_l^2)$ for a measurement $m$ and prior $\mathcal N(0, \sigma_p^2)$, show that the posterior is Gaussian with mean $\frac{\sigma_p^2}{\sigma_p^2 + \sigma_l^2}m$ and variance $\frac{\sigma_p^2\sigma_l^2}{\sigma_p^2 + \sigma_l^2}$.">

The log-posterior is, up to a constant, $-\frac{(s - m)^2}{2\sigma_l^2} - \frac{s^2}{2\sigma_p^2}$, a quadratic in $s$, so the posterior is Gaussian.

Its precision is the coefficient of $-s^2/2$: $\frac{1}{\sigma_l^2} + \frac{1}{\sigma_p^2}$, so its variance is $\frac{\sigma_p^2\sigma_l^2}{\sigma_p^2 + \sigma_l^2}$.

Setting the derivative to zero, $\frac{m - s}{\sigma_l^2} = \frac{s}{\sigma_p^2}$, so $s = \frac{\sigma_p^2}{\sigma_p^2 + \sigma_l^2}m$. The estimate shrinks towards zero by a factor that approaches 0 as $\sigma_l$ grows, which is the slowing at low contrast.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. 2AFC and d'" task="Show that if signal and noise responses are independent with $\mathcal N(d', 1)$ and $\mathcal N(0, 1)$, the probability that the signal response exceeds the noise response is $\Phi(d'/\sqrt2)$.">

The difference $D = x_s - x_n$ is normal with mean $d'$ and variance $1 + 1 = 2$, since the responses are independent.

$P(D \gt 0) = P\left(\frac{D - d'}{\sqrt2} \gt -\frac{d'}{\sqrt2}\right) = 1 - \Phi\left(-\frac{d'}{\sqrt2}\right) = \Phi\left(\frac{d'}{\sqrt2}\right)$.

So a 2AFC observer with $d' = 1$ is 76% correct, and the area under the yes-no ROC, which is the same probability, is also 0.76.

</Prove>
