<Step n={1} title="Four models of the prior" minutes={30} />

All four models share the same decision stage, a logistic function of the stimulus and a bias, and differ only in the prior term $\pi_t$ added to it:

$$
P(\text{right}_t) = \sigma\!\left(w_c f(c_t) + b + w_p\,\pi_t\right).
$$

**No prior.** $\pi_t = 0$. Two parameters, $w_c$ and $b$.

**Bayesian observer.** The mouse knows that the block is either a right block (stimulus on the right with probability 0.8) or a left block, and that blocks switch with a small probability $h$ on each trial. After each trial's feedback it knows where the stimulus was. Its belief $q_t$ that the current block is a right block is then updated exactly as in the hidden Markov models of Week 50, with the switch probability playing the role of the transition matrix. Before trial $t$ the predicted probability that the stimulus is on the right is $0.8\,q_t + 0.2\,(1 - q_t)$, and $\pi_t$ is its log-odds. The mouse's switch rate is fixed at $h = 0.02$, close to the true average of one switch every 50 trials, so the model has three parameters: $w_c$, $b$ and $w_p$. The same filter is the simplest case of the online change-point detection of Adams and MacKay (2007).

**Stimulus kernel.** $\pi_t$ is an exponentially weighted average of past stimulus sides $s_{t'} = \pm 1$: $\pi_t = (1 - \alpha)\,\pi_{t-1} + \alpha\, s_{t-1}$. Four parameters, adding the rate $\alpha$.

**Action kernel.** The same, but averaging the mouse's own past choices $a_{t'} = \pm 1$ instead of the stimulus sides. Four parameters.

The last two differ only after errors, and Day 2 has already hinted which way the data lean.

The observer's prior averages +0.94 in right blocks and −0.95 in left blocks, out of a possible ±1.39 (the log-odds of 0.8). It does not reach the maximum because every block starts with the observer still believing in the previous one, and it needs several trials of evidence to change its mind. In the unbiased first 90 trials of each session it hovers around zero, as it should. This is what an ideal mouse that knew the rules of the task would expect on every trial.

Evidence is approximated by $-\text{BIC}/2$, as in Week 50.

Both are far ahead of the model with no prior, by about 3,800 nats: whatever the mice do, they do track the block.

Random effects agree: the expected frequency of the action kernel in the population is 0.86, and its exceedance probability is effectively 1, since it is the most common model in every one of the 20,000 samples. The other three models share the remaining 0.14 almost equally, and most of that is the Dirichlet prior's pull towards equal frequencies with only 18 mice, not evidence for them. For the report, the claim is: most IBL mice in this task behave as though they keep a leaky running average of their own recent choices, not as Bayesian observers of the block.

The fitted rates have a median of 0.21, a memory of about five trials, and range from 0.08 to 0.34.
