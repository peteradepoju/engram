<Step n={1} title="The dual problem" minutes={25} />

<Derivation title="From the soft-margin primal to its dual">
<div>

Write the soft-margin SVM with slack variables: minimise $\frac12\lVert\mathbf w\rVert^2 + C\sum_i\xi_i$ subject to $y_i(\mathbf w^\top\mathbf x_i + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i$ and $\xi_i \ge 0$. Introduce multipliers $\alpha_i \ge 0$ and $\mu_i \ge 0$ for the two sets of constraints. The Lagrangian is

$$
\mathcal L = \tfrac12\lVert\mathbf w\rVert^2 + C\sum_i\xi_i - \sum_i\alpha_i\left[y_i(\mathbf w^\top\mathbf x_i + b) - 1 + \xi_i\right] - \sum_i\mu_i\xi_i.
$$

</div>
<div>

Setting its derivatives to zero: $\partial/\partial\mathbf w$ gives $\mathbf w = \sum_i\alpha_iy_i\mathbf x_i$; $\partial/\partial b$ gives $\sum_i\alpha_iy_i = 0$; $\partial/\partial\xi_i$ gives $\alpha_i = C - \mu_i$, so $0 \le \alpha_i \le C$.

</div>
<div>

Substituting back eliminates $\mathbf w$, $b$ and $\boldsymbol\xi$, leaving the **dual**:

$$
\max_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\ \sum_i\alpha_i - \tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_jy_iy_j\,\mathbf x_i^\top\mathbf x_j \quad\text{subject to}\quad 0 \le \alpha_i \le C,\ \sum_i\alpha_iy_i = 0.
$$

The data enter only through $\mathbf x_i^\top\mathbf x_j$, so a kernel can replace them, and the classifier becomes $f(\mathbf x) = \sum_i\alpha_iy_ik(\mathbf x_i, \mathbf x) + b$.

</div>
</Derivation>

The optimality (KKT) conditions sort the examples into three groups: $\alpha_i = 0$ for examples beyond the margin ($y_if \ge 1$), $0 \lt \alpha_i \lt C$ for those exactly on it ($y_if = 1$), and $\alpha_i = C$ for those inside it or misclassified ($y_if \le 1$). The last two groups are the support vectors.

<Step n={2} title="Solving the dual" minutes={30} />

A simple and effective solver updates one $\alpha_i$ at a time, holding the others fixed, and clips it to $[0, C]$. The equality constraint $\sum_i\alpha_iy_i = 0$ comes from the bias $b$; a common trick removes it by folding the bias into the kernel (adding a constant 1 to every kernel value), after which each coordinate update is a one-dimensional quadratic with a closed-form solution.

On overlapping classes with $C = 1$, 90 of the 200 examples are support vectors, 85 of them at the bound $C$: they sit inside the margin or on the wrong side, as overlapping classes must. The recovered weights, (0.98, 0.73), give 84.5% test accuracy, the same as the primal solver yesterday on similar data. The KKT checks pass: every example with $\alpha_i = 0$ has a margin of at least 1, and every one at $C$ has a margin of at most 1. With overlap, support vectors are numerous; the sparsity of the SVM is greatest when classes are well separated.

A linear boundary gets 85.7% of the crescents right, because it cuts straight through both tips. The Gaussian kernel follows their curves and reaches 98.4%, from only 16 support vectors, 8% of the training set, which answers the prediction. The classifier is a sum of 16 Gaussian bumps centred on the hardest examples; the other 184 could be deleted without changing a single prediction. Fewer support vectors also means faster prediction, since each new example is compared only with them.

The grid shows the expected structure. With $\gamma = 0.05$ the kernel is too broad and the boundary nearly straight (82% to 89%); with $\gamma = 100$ and small $C$ the kernel is so narrow that the SVM fails badly (55%), and even with large $C$ it plateaus at 97%. A broad ridge of good settings runs through $\gamma$ between 0.5 and 10 for $C$ of 1 or more, where cross-validated accuracy reaches 99 to 100%. The chosen pair, $C = 1$ and $\gamma = 2$, gives 98.4% on the test set. As so often, many settings are nearly equally good, and the cross-validated optimum is one point on a plateau, not a sharp peak.

<Key title="Before you go">
<ul>
<li>The SVM dual has one variable per example and uses the data only through a kernel.</li>
<li>Coordinate ascent with clipping solves it simply; the KKT conditions identify the support vectors.</li>
<li>A Gaussian kernel turns the same solver into a flexible nonlinear classifier.</li>
<li>C and γ must be chosen together, by cross-validation.</li>
</ul>
