The random forest reaches 91.5% and the boosted trees 93%, against a noise ceiling of about 94%. Neither ensemble was told the form of the tuning, which is their real advantage as encoding models: they set a target that a hand-built model of the neuron should reach before anyone believes it is complete.

**Part B: AdaBoost's training error falls exponentially.** Freund and Schapire proved that after $T$ rounds the training error of AdaBoost is at most $\prod_t 2\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}$. If every stump has error at most $\frac12 - \gamma$, each factor is at most $\sqrt{1 - 4\gamma^2}$, so the bound falls exponentially in $T$. Check the bound, round by round, on the ring problem of Day 4.

The bound holds at every round and is far from tight: after 10 rounds the training error is 0.167 against a bound of 0.654, and after 100 rounds 0.003 against 0.125. The bound tracks the exponential loss, which punishes confident errors heavily, rather than the error itself. Its message is qualitative: as long as each stump beats chance by some margin, the training error must go to zero, exponentially fast. That is why weak learners suffice, and also why AdaBoost, with noisy labels, will eventually fit the noise unless it is stopped.

<Prove title="1. AdaBoost's vote minimises exponential loss" task="AdaBoost adds $\alpha h(\mathbf x)$ to the ensemble, with $h(\mathbf x) = \pm1$, to reduce the exponential loss $\sum_i w_i e^{-\alpha y_ih(\mathbf x_i)}$, where the weights $w_i$ sum to 1 and $\epsilon$ is the total weight of the examples $h$ misclassifies. Show that the best $\alpha$ is $\frac12\log\frac{1 - \epsilon}{\epsilon}$.">

Split the sum into correctly classified examples, where $y_ih(\mathbf x_i) = 1$, and misclassified ones, where it is $-1$:

$$
L(\alpha) = (1 - \epsilon)e^{-\alpha} + \epsilon e^{\alpha}.
$$

Differentiate and set to zero: $-(1 - \epsilon)e^{-\alpha} + \epsilon e^{\alpha} = 0$, so $e^{2\alpha} = (1 - \epsilon)/\epsilon$ and $\alpha = \frac12\log\frac{1 - \epsilon}{\epsilon}$. The second derivative is positive, so this is a minimum.

At this $\alpha$, $L = 2\sqrt{\epsilon(1 - \epsilon)}$: the loss is multiplied by this factor each round, which is the bound of Part B, since the exponential loss is never smaller than the training error.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. Pseudo-residuals for logistic loss" task="For a label $y \in \{0, 1\}$ and a score $F$ with $p = \sigma(F) = 1/(1 + e^{-F})$, the logistic loss is $L = -y\log p - (1 - y)\log(1 - p)$. Show that the pseudo-residual $-\partial L/\partial F$ is $y - p$.">

Use $\partial p/\partial F = p(1 - p)$ (Week 56). Then

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial F} = \left(-\frac yp + \frac{1 - y}{1 - p}\right)p(1 - p) = -y(1 - p) + (1 - y)p = p - y.
$$

So $-\partial L/\partial F = y - p$: gradient boosting for classification fits each tree to the difference between the label and the current predicted probability, the same form as the residual for squared loss.

</Prove>
