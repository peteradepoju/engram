<Step n={1} title="The cocktail party" minutes={20} />

The model is $\mathbf x = A\mathbf s$: $d$ recorded channels are a fixed linear mixture of $d$ sources. Neither $A$ nor $\mathbf s$ is known. Whitening the data (Week 39) makes the channels uncorrelated with unit variance, $\mathbf z = V\mathbf x$, but leaves a problem: if $\mathbf z$ is white, so is $R\mathbf z$ for any rotation $R$. Second-order statistics, and therefore PCA, cannot choose among rotations. **Independent component analysis** chooses the rotation that makes the outputs statistically independent, which needs higher-order statistics. By the central limit theorem, a mixture of independent sources is more Gaussian than the sources themselves, so the rotation that makes each output as non-Gaussian as possible undoes the mixing. Hyvärinen and Oja (2000), today's paper, review this idea and the fast fixed-point algorithm, FastICA, that made ICA practical.

<Step n={2} title="FastICA" minutes={35} />

FastICA finds one unmixing direction $\mathbf w$ at a time in whitened data, maximising the non-Gaussianity of $\mathbf w^\top\mathbf z$ measured by a contrast function such as $G(u) = \log\cosh u$. Its fixed-point update, derived as a Newton step, is

$$
\mathbf w \leftarrow E\left[\mathbf z\,g(\mathbf w^\top\mathbf z)\right] - E\left[g'(\mathbf w^\top\mathbf z)\right]\mathbf w,\qquad g = \tanh,
$$

followed by normalising $\mathbf w$. To find several components, **deflation** removes from each new $\mathbf w$ its projections on the directions already found, so that the components are orthogonal in the whitened space.

Each true source is matched by one component with an absolute correlation of 0.998 or more, though not in the original order: the event train came out second and the oscillation first. Nothing about the mixing matrix was used. The recorded channels each look like a messy blend, while the components are clean: the events, the 7 Hz rhythm, the sawtooth. The event train is the most non-Gaussian (heavy-tailed), the sawtooth and the sine are less so but in the other direction (light-tailed), and the $\log\cosh$ contrast handles both.

<Step n={3} title="What ICA cannot do" minutes={20} />

ICA recovers the sources only up to permutation and scale, including sign: $A\mathbf s = (AP^{-1}D^{-1})(DP\mathbf s)$ for any permutation $P$ and diagonal $D$. And at most one source may be Gaussian. A Gaussian vector with identity covariance looks the same in every rotated frame, so if two sources are Gaussian, any rotation of them is an equally good answer, and the data contain no information about which is right.

With the non-Gaussian sources every start recovers the first source perfectly. With Gaussian sources the answer depends on the start, with correlations from 0.77 to 0.95: FastICA converges to whatever rotation the noise in this finite sample happens to favour, and a different sample would favour a different one. In practice the warning sign is instability: components that change from run to run, or from one half of the data to the other, are not identified. Tools such as ICASSO rerun ICA many times and keep only components that recur.

<Step n={4} title="Removing an artefact" minutes={30} />

The most common use of ICA in neuroscience is cleaning EEG and MEG: eye blinks, eye movements and heartbeats spread across many sensors, but each comes from one source, so it appears as one independent component (Jung and colleagues, 2000). Identify that component, set it to zero, and project back to the sensors with the mixing matrix $\hat A = W^{-1}$.

The blink component stands out at once, with a kurtosis of 23.5 against at most 5.5 for the others: blinks are rare, large and brief, the very picture of a heavy-tailed source. Zeroing it removes 99.8% of the blink's share of channel variance, and the cleaned frontal channel correlates at 0.988 with what that channel would have recorded without blinks. In real EEG the blink is rarely this clean a single component, since more sources than channels and slight nonstationarity smear it, and components are usually chosen by inspecting their scalp maps and time courses rather than by kurtosis alone.

<Key title="Before you go">
<ul>
<li>Whitening fixes everything but a rotation; independence fixes the rotation.</li>
<li>FastICA maximises non-Gaussianity one component at a time.</li>
<li>Sources are recovered up to order, scale and sign, and only if at most one is Gaussian.</li>
<li>ICA removes artefacts that come from one source spread over many channels.</li>
</ul>
