<Step n={1} title="Parts and wholes" minutes={20} />

Arrange a calcium imaging movie as a matrix $X$ with one row per frame and one column per pixel. NMF approximates it as

$$
X \approx WH,\qquad W \ge 0,\ H \ge 0,
$$

with $W$ ($T \times k$) holding the activity of $k$ components over time and $H$ ($k \times P$) their spatial footprints. Because nothing can be subtracted, each frame is a sum of footprints scaled by non-negative activities, which is exactly how a movie of cells is made. Lee and Seung (1999), today's paper, contrasted NMF with PCA and vector quantisation on images of faces: PCA found holistic "eigenfaces" with positive and negative parts, while NMF found localised parts such as noses and eyebrows. The same constraint underlies the cell-finding methods used in calcium imaging today, such as CNMF (Pnevmatikakis and colleagues, 2016).

<Step n={2} title="Multiplicative updates" minutes={30} />

<Derivation title="Gradient descent with a clever step size">
<div>

Minimise $\lVert X - WH\rVert_F^2$ over $H \ge 0$ with $W$ fixed. The gradient with respect to $H$ is $2(W^\top WH - W^\top X)$, so gradient descent with a step $\eta_{kj}$ for each entry would be $H_{kj} \leftarrow H_{kj} + \eta_{kj}\left[(W^\top X)_{kj} - (W^\top WH)_{kj}\right]$.

</div>
<div>

Lee and Seung (2001) chose the step size $\eta_{kj} = H_{kj}/(W^\top WH)_{kj}$, which turns the update into a multiplication:

$$
H \leftarrow H \odot \frac{W^\top X}{W^\top WH},\qquad W \leftarrow W \odot \frac{XH^\top}{WHH^\top},
$$

with $\odot$ and the fraction taken element by element.

</div>
<div>

Every factor in the update is non-negative, so non-negativity is kept automatically, with no projection. They proved that the error never increases. The updates are simple but can be slow, and an entry that reaches zero stays at zero, so initialisation matters.

</div>
</Derivation>

The principal components match single cells far less well, with correlations from 0.67 to 0.82, and a quarter of their total weight is negative. Because three cells share some events, the direction of greatest variance is their sum, and the next components must be orthogonal to it, so they become differences between cells: one cell positive, its neighbour negative. These components are a perfectly good basis for the movie, but no single one of them is a cell. Non-negativity rules out the cancellation that PCA relies on.

<Step n={4} title="Background and uniqueness" minutes={25} />

Real movies contain **neuropil**: diffuse fluorescence from axons and dendrites that fluctuates on its own and covers the whole field. If NMF has no component for it, the background is shared out among the cells, contaminating their activity. Giving NMF one extra component lets the background take its own factor. NMF solutions are also not unique in general: if footprints overlap heavily, other non-negative factorisations fit as well, and the answer depends on the start. Methods for imaging add constraints, such as sparsity, spatial locality and models of calcium dynamics, to pin the answer down.
