<Step n={1} title="Poisson observations" minutes={35} />

The **Poisson linear dynamical system** (PLDS; Macke and colleagues, 2011, today's paper) keeps the linear Gaussian dynamics, $\mathbf z_t = A\mathbf z_{t-1} + \mathbf w_t$, but emits spike counts with rates $\lambda_{tn} = \exp(\mathbf c_n^\top\mathbf z_t + d_n)$. The posterior over the latent path is no longer Gaussian, so inference uses a **Laplace approximation**: find the mode of the log posterior over the whole trajectory by Newton's method, then take the Hessian there as the posterior precision. Because the log prior's precision is block-tridiagonal and the likelihood contributes only block-diagonal terms, each Newton step costs time linear in the length of the recording.

Newton's method raises the log posterior from $-6782$ to $-4493$ and converges in a handful of steps, and the inferred path spans almost exactly the same subspace as the true one (agreement 0.986) from a population firing at 0.3 spikes per bin, where 60% of bins are empty. The Hessian is the sum of a block-tridiagonal prior precision and a block-diagonal likelihood term, so it is itself block-tridiagonal: solving with it costs time linear in the recording, which is what makes the method practical for long sessions. Its inverse also gives the posterior covariance, needed for the M step when the model is learned rather than given.

<Step n={3} title="Switching dynamics" minutes={35} />

A **switching linear dynamical system** has a discrete state $s_t$, evolving as a Markov chain, that selects which linear dynamics govern the continuous state. Exact inference is intractable, because the posterior over the continuous state becomes a mixture with a number of components growing exponentially in time, so practical methods approximate (assumed density filtering, variational inference, or sampling). When the continuous state is directly observed, as in a behavioural time series, the regimes can be inferred exactly with the forward-backward algorithm of Day 1, with the emission likelihood of each regime given by its one-step prediction.

<Step n={4} title="Checks" minutes={15} />

A state-space model of a population should pass three checks before its states or dynamics are interpreted. **Co-smoothing** (Day 4): can it predict neurons it never saw, from latents inferred from the rest? **Simulation**: do data generated by the fitted model look like the real data in statistics it was not fitted to, such as interspike intervals, pairwise correlations or dwell times? **Stability**: do the latent dimensionality, the eigenvalues and the segmentation survive refitting on halves of the data or on other sessions? Models with many parameters and flexible dynamics can fit anything, and these checks are what separate a description from an artefact.
