**Part A: a particle filter for a circular variable.** A population of head-direction cells reports an angle that drifts as a random walk on the circle, and each cell fires Poisson spikes with a von Mises tuning curve. The state is circular and the observations are counts, so neither the Kalman filter nor a discrete HMM is natural. A **particle filter** represents the posterior by weighted samples: propagate each particle through the dynamics, weight it by the likelihood of the new observation, and resample when the weights become uneven.

The particle filter tracks head direction with a mean circular error of 0.068 radians, about 4 degrees, against 0.088 for the population vector computed bin by bin. The gain comes from the dynamics: an angle cannot jump far in one bin, so an implausible population vector is overruled by the particles' prior. The filter resampled on 227 of 400 bins, whenever the weights became concentrated on a few particles. Nothing here is linear or Gaussian, and the same code would work for a bimodal posterior, which a Kalman filter cannot represent at all.

**Part B: discrete or continuous?** Given a recording, is the latent state a few discrete levels or a continuous variable? One fair comparison keeps the observation model fixed and varies only the state space: fit Poisson HMMs with 2 to 8 states and look at the held-out likelihood. Truly discrete data are explained by as many states as there are levels and gain nothing from more; a continuous latent needs several, because each state is one quantisation level of the continuum.

For the two-level data, held-out likelihood is flat from two states onwards: $-16.191$ at $K = 2$ and no better at 8, so the parsimonious choice is 2, the truth. For the drifting latent, it improves steeply from $-30.5$ at $K = 2$ to $-24.7$ at $K = 4$ and then levels off: a continuum is approximated by quantising it, and four levels capture most of what twelve noisy neurons can resolve. The shape of the curve is the diagnostic. A sharp plateau after a few states suggests genuine discrete states; a slow climb suggests a continuous variable being chopped up, and a continuous-state model will describe it better and more compactly.

<Prove title="1. The Kalman update by Gaussian conditioning" task="With prior $\mathbf z \sim \mathcal N(\mathbf m^-, P^-)$ and observation $\mathbf y = C\mathbf z + \mathbf v$, $\mathbf v \sim \mathcal N(\mathbf 0, R)$ independent of $\mathbf z$, derive the posterior mean and covariance of $\mathbf z$ given $\mathbf y$.">

$(\mathbf z, \mathbf y)$ is jointly Gaussian, since $\mathbf y$ is a linear function of $\mathbf z$ and $\mathbf v$. Its blocks are $\text{Cov}(\mathbf z) = P^-$, $\text{Cov}(\mathbf z, \mathbf y) = P^-C^\top$ and $\text{Cov}(\mathbf y) = CP^-C^\top + R$.

Gaussian conditioning gives

$$
E[\mathbf z \mid \mathbf y] = \mathbf m^- + P^-C^\top(CP^-C^\top + R)^{-1}(\mathbf y - C\mathbf m^-),
$$

$$
\text{Cov}[\mathbf z \mid \mathbf y] = P^- - P^-C^\top(CP^-C^\top + R)^{-1}CP^-.
$$

Writing $K = P^-C^\top(CP^-C^\top + R)^{-1}$ gives the familiar $\mathbf m = \mathbf m^- + K(\mathbf y - C\mathbf m^-)$ and $P = (I - KC)P^-$. Nothing is approximated: the Kalman filter is exact Gaussian conditioning, applied one observation at a time.

</Prove>

<Prove title="2. The likelihood from the innovations" task="Show that $\log p(\mathbf y_{1:T}) = \sum_{t=1}^T\log\mathcal N\left(\mathbf y_t; C\mathbf m_t^-, CP_t^-C^\top + R\right)$.">

By the chain rule, $p(\mathbf y_{1:T}) = \prod_tp(\mathbf y_t \mid \mathbf y_{1:t-1})$.

Each factor is the predictive distribution of the next observation given the past. Given $\mathbf y_{1:t-1}$, the state is $\mathcal N(\mathbf m_t^-, P_t^-)$ by the predict step, and $\mathbf y_t = C\mathbf z_t + \mathbf v_t$ is a linear function of it plus independent noise, so it is Gaussian with mean $C\mathbf m_t^-$ and covariance $CP_t^-C^\top + R$.

Taking logarithms and summing gives the result: the filter produces the likelihood as a by-product, which is what EM and model comparison need. The innovations $\mathbf y_t - C\mathbf m_t^-$ are also independent of each other, which gives a useful diagnostic: if a fitted model is right, its innovations should be white.

</Prove>
