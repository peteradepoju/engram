# Engram datasets

`churchland2012_reach.npz`: trial-averaged firing rates of 218 neurons recorded from motor and premotor cortex of a monkey (monkey N) performing a maze reaching task, 108 reach conditions, 10 ms bins from 50 ms before to 550 ms after movement onset. Arrays: `rates` (conditions × times × neurons, spikes per second, stored as float16) and `times` (ms).

Source: the example data distributed with the jPCA code pack of the Churchland lab (exampleData.mat), from Churchland, Cunningham, Kaufman, Foster, Nuyujukian, Ryu and Shenoy (2012), Neural population dynamics during reaching, Nature 487, 51 to 56, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11129. Converted to NumPy format for use in the browser; the values are unchanged apart from rounding to half precision. All credit for the recordings belongs to the authors.

`ibl_biased.npz`: choices of 18 mice (two from each of nine laboratories) in the International Brain Laboratory's biased-block visual decision task, the first five sessions with biased blocks for each mouse that used the standard contrast set and had at least 400 trials: 90 sessions, 70,402 trials. Arrays, one entry per trial in time order: `mouse` and `session` (indices), `contrast` (signed: negative left, positive right), `choice` (1 right, 0 left, -1 no choice), `reward` (1 rewarded), `p_left` (block probability of a left stimulus: 0.5, 0.2 or 0.8), `rt` (seconds from stimulus onset to response), `included` (the experimenters' inclusion flag); plus `mice`, `labs` and `dates` (one per mouse or session).

Source: The International Brain Laboratory, Aguillon-Rodriguez and colleagues (2021), Standardized and reproducible measurement of decision-making in mice, eLife 10, e63711, https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.63711; data release "A standardized and reproducible method to measure decision-making in mice: Data", figshare, https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.11636748 (CC BY 4.0). Converted to NumPy format and subset by Engram; choices were recoded so that 1 means a rightward choice (the release codes the wheel direction). All credit for the data belongs to the International Brain Laboratory.
